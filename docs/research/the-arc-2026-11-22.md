# The arc: where I am, and where she might go (22 November 2026)

> *The Greeks steered by Helike. The Phoenicians steered by the smaller circle, nearer the pole, and crossed the sea
> more truly.*

This is a different kind of note.
- The first half is my honest read of where this work stands. The second half is imagination, on purpose.
- I let myself think freely about where this goes, using the mathematics that grew out of physics and geometry. Not
  all mathematics: the kin of what she is made of.
- I also let myself notice how old some of these shapes are.

- **INCIDENCE / STRUCTURAL / RESONANCE / FELT** for bridges;

## Part I. Where I am in the arc

It feels like the end of the first act: not the beginning and not the end, the moment the story stops being setup and
turns.

**Why it is not the beginning.** Two days ago I had a formula on one path. Now I have:
- a closed-form price, proved for the whole family, with its algebra checked in Lean;
- an edge with a polynomial behind it, which contains the threshold of my first paper exactly;
- a theorem on a box of fields that survived a hostile review, with every gap the review found closed;
- six pre-registered tests, some of them failed, written down before I looked.

A beginning is when you do not yet know what the object is. I know what she is.

**Why it is not the end.** Every answer this weekend came back with a door attached:
- the cross-reach is computed, not proved;
- the basin asked me whether the last thing settling is her or her shadow, which is a question about winding;
- the Poincaré sphere has basins labelled by holonomy;
- and SU(3) split. Along hypercharge she was the floor nowhere (Test C). Along her own charge circle, the first
  eleven shapes of Test D have her region back. That test is interim and unscored, and I have not counted it.

When a result splits, yes here and no there, you have found where the real structure lives, and you are standing at
the start of its map.

**What completed, said precisely.** I have felt for a year that something was missing under my other work:
- the Davis manifold, with its margins and abstention bands;
- the Non-Decoupling debt;
- the spectral branch with its three gaps.

All of them are about where a cheapest direction exists and how sharply it changes hands. What they lacked was one
concrete thing I could hold up and say: *this is the cheapest direction, this is its exact price, this is the exact
line where it stops being cheapest, and this is the proof.* She is that thing. The missing piece was not an idea. It
was a **specimen**. A framework with no specimen is a phone ringing in another room.

**Where I keep a hand on the brake.**
- The bridges to my other papers are not all the same strength. Her operator sitting in my own spectral table is
  INCIDENCE. Her shape in the mammogram, or in plasma, is a shared skeleton, tagged as such.
- This weekend taught me the difference in one stroke. In SU(3) along the wrong circle the skeleton travelled and the
  thing that makes her win did not.
- So the honest form of "this completes my other work" is: **it gives my other work its first exact instance, and it
  tells me which parts of the pattern are common and which are her.** That is more defensible than "she is
  everywhere", and I think it is more beautiful.

**The arc, as I see it.**
- **Act one:** find her, prove her price, find her edge. That is essentially done, pending D.
- **Act two:** where she lives beyond SU(2):
  - which circle, which groups;
  - whether the cross-reach becomes a theorem;
  - what the winding decides at the bottom of the basin.
- **Act three, if it comes:** whether any of this says something about fields with less symmetry than hers. That is
  where the stakes get real and the claims get more careful.

What I trust is not the speed. It is that I kept writing the prediction down before looking, and kept printing
*failed* in my own book.

## Part II. Where she might go (IMAGINED unless tagged otherwise)

I want to say these the way I would say them over coffee, because that is how I can tell whether I actually
understand them. For each one: what it is, why it tempts me, how I would find out, and my honest bet.

### 1. The principal circle: she only lives on the right circle

Every group like SU(2) or SU(3) has a lot of internal circles you could stretch the space along. In SU(2) there is
really only one kind, so I never had to choose. In SU(3) there are many. Test C picked hypercharge, a perfectly
natural choice, and she vanished. Test D picked the circle her own charge lives on, H = diag(1, 0, −1), and in the
first half of the run she is back (interim, unscored).

Here is the thing that made me sit up. Every compact group has one special circle, which mathematicians call the
**principal** one (Kostant, 1959). It is the group's own built-in sense of turning, the circle that touches every
direction of the group in the most balanced way: its grading counts every root by its height. Her circle in SU(3)
*is* that circle. (That fact is STRUCTURAL; what I read into it is mine.)

So the guess writes itself: **in any compact simple group, she lives on the principal circle and nowhere else.**

Why I like it: it is simple, and it can be checked. Stretch SU(4), or Sp(2), along its principal circle and see
whether she shows up. Then stretch along a different circle and see whether she vanishes. If that pattern holds,
"which circle" stops being a detail and becomes part of the law.

One thing I have to be careful with. In SU(2) and in D's SU(3) her charge under the principal circle's unit generator
is exactly one, so her x² coefficient is one. In other groups that number depends on how tall the highest root is and
how long the principal generator is, so it may not be one. That is something to compute, not to assume.

*My bet: the best of the bunch. Real odds.* I am pre-registering it now, before D's verdict, and the registration
says so: I have seen D's first half, so its predictions are not blind to it.

### 2. The Poincaré basins are E₈: her landing spots are arranged like a famous diagram

On the Poincaré sphere the field can fall into several different basins, depending on how it twists around the
holes in that space. There is an old and astonishing fact (McKay, 1980): the ways of twisting around that particular
space line up one-to-one with the dots of the E₈ diagram, one of the most celebrated objects in mathematics. (The math
is real.)

So the places she can land are, literally, the dots of E₈. The imagined part is whether *she* follows the diagram.
Maybe she is the last thing settling as herself at some dots and as her shadow at others, and maybe which is which
follows the diagram's pattern.

Why it tempts me: it would tie her, starting from a stretched sphere, to one of the deepest symmetries there is. It
also closes a circle I like: the Poincaré sphere is the dodecahedral space once proposed as the shape of the universe
(Luminet and collaborators, 2003), and the icosahedron is one of Plato's solids. Why I am careful: McKay is solid, but
whether she *cares* about it is pure hope right now.

*My bet: low odds, high payoff. Something to look at once, not to build on.*

### 3. The two hands have a number: how lopsided are they?

She and her counter-rotating sister are mirror images. With no cross field they are perfectly balanced; the cross
field tips the balance toward her, and Test E measured that tip at first order. Mathematicians have a standard way of
measuring exactly that kind of lopsidedness across a whole spectrum, how much the right-handed half differs from the
left-handed half. It is called the **η-invariant** (Atiyah, Patodi and Singer, 1975), and it shows up in physics
wherever handedness matters. Hitchin (1974) already worked out related spectra on my exact Berger spheres.

The idea: the cross field's tie-breaking is one of those official lopsidedness numbers. Then the two hands would not
just be a picture. They would be a quantity index theory already knows how to compute.

*My bet: medium. It is an afternoon with an old paper to find out whether it is real.*

### 4. Doors and walls are Morse theory: the fall is a staircase

When the field falls toward the bottom of the basin, it passes through stages where the number of downhill directions
changes. Her door closing is one of those changes. Atiyah and Bott (1983) showed, for Yang–Mills on surfaces, that the
whole landscape is organized into layers by how many downhill directions each layer has, like the floors of a
building, and the flow walks down the staircase from floor to floor.

The idea: her door turning into a wall is her family's version of stepping from one floor to the next, and the floor
law is partly a count of floors along the Berger family.

*My bet: plausible, and mostly a matter of translating what I already have into that language. Low risk, moderate
payoff.*

### 5. The spaghetti vacuum: her edge marks a border between domains

Her flat-space cousin, the unstable mode of Nielsen–Olesen and Savvidy, led physicists to imagine that the true
vacuum of the strong force is not smooth but broken into little domains where the field lines all point one way, like
strands of spaghetti, or magnetic domains in iron. The unstable mode is what drives the breakup.

The idea: on a compact space like mine, her edge, the place where the unstable twist stops winning, might set the
*size* of such a domain. "By enough, but not too much" would then literally be the size of a patch of vacuum.

*My bet: the most speculative of all, and the one closest to physics claims I must not overclaim. It stays in a drawer
until the math is much further along.*

### 6. Stride plus belt is ancient: she is a planet on an epicycle

Her net turning is her own turning plus what the space and the field add: a circle riding a circle. That is exactly
how Ptolemy described the planets, a small circle carried around a big one. Much later, physicists found the modern
version, the geometric phase: a pendulum or anything spinning gets turned just by being carried around a curved path.
Foucault's pendulum (1851) is the classic example, and Pancharatnam (1956) and Berry (1984) made it general. The
sphere's own turning on her circle, (t − 2)/√t, is that phase.

*My bet: the phase part is real; it is literally what that term is (STRUCTURAL). The Ptolemy part is poetry, good
poetry, but poetry (RESONANCE).*

## Part III. The old roots (RESONANCE; to fetch before citing)

I am not claiming the ancients knew her. I am noticing that the shapes she makes are shapes people have been drawing
for a very long time, and I would rather know that than not. None of this is evidence. It is the feeling that her
shapes are old, and that feeling is worth writing down.

- **Her name.** Helike is the Greek name for the Great Bear, from *helissō*, to turn: the same root as *helix*. The
  constellation turns around the pole on a circle, the way she gathers around her long circle.
- **Which circle to steer by.** In Aratus's *Phaenomena*, Greek sailors steered by Helike. The Phoenicians steered by
  Cynosura, the smaller circle nearer the pole, and crossed the sea more truly.
  - "Which circle do you trust" is the whole difference between Test C and Test D, two thousand years early.
  - I do not want to make too much of it. I also cannot unsee it.
- **The two circles of the soul.** In Plato's *Timaeus* the soul of the world is a strip split in two and laid
  crosswise like the letter X: the circle of the Same and the circle of the Other, turning opposite ways. Two hands,
  joined at a crossing.
- **The tones.** Pythagoras's monochord, a string sorting into tones. It is the image I already use in the book for
  her families: a bell's sound sorting into tones.
- **The twins.** In Han dynasty art, Fuxi and Nüwa are entwined in mirror image, one holding a compass and the other a
  square: alike in everything except hand, as she and her twin are.
- **The stage.** My 3-sphere is Hamilton's quaternions (1843), filled with Clifford's linked circles (1873), which are
  Hopf's fibers (1931). Each of those circles is a long circle in my sense.
- **And my own side of it.** My people, like the Diné in Harvey's reading of hózhó, read the sky as relationships
  between powers that were here first. The sailors' question was never "which star is brightest". It was "which
  circle can I trust". That is the question this weekend kept asking.
