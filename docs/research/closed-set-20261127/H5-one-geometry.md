Throughout, $G=SU(2)$ (H7 carries the extension), $\beta_0 = b_0 = 22/3$ and $\beta_1 = 34N^2/3 = 136/3$ are the one- and two-loop coefficients of $\mu\,dg/d\mu = -\beta_0 g^3/(16\pi^2) - \beta_1 g^5/(16\pi^2)^2 - \dots$ (Caswell 1974; Jones 1974). ## 1. Statement (high fidelity)

### 1.1 Objects

**Geometry.** Fix $t>0$ ($t=1$ is the round case). Let $h_t$ be the left-invariant Berger metric on $S^3 = SU(2)$ used in lines 7-9, normalized so that $h_1$ is the round metric of radius 1. For $R>0$ put
$$M_R = S^3\times\mathbb R,\qquad g_R = R^2 h_t + d\tau^2,\qquad \Theta:(x,\tau)\mapsto(x,-\tau).$$
Every principal $SU(2)$ bundle over $M_R$ is trivial ($BSU(2)$ is 3-connected and $M_R$ retracts onto $S^3$), and $S^3$ is simply connected, so there are no 't Hooft flux sectors, unlike on the torus.

**Observables.** $\mathcal O(M)$ is the algebra generated by
- Wilson loops $W_C = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\,\mathrm{Hol}_C$ for piecewise smooth loops $C\subset M$;
- flowed densities $E_{s}(f) = \int_M f\,\operatorname{tr}(G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu})(s,\cdot)\,d\mathrm{vol}$, with $f\in C_c^\infty(M)$ and $G$ the curvature of the Yang-Mills gradient flow of the metric of $M$ at flow time $s>0$ (Lüscher 2010; finiteness to all orders, Lüscher and Weisz 2011). Flow time $s$ is in physical units.

These are gauge invariant and need no renormalization beyond the coupling. I use them because "same renormalized theory" must be tested on observables that are finite in every regularization; bare $\operatorname{tr}F^2(x)$ is not (that is H6's business).

**Admissible sphere regulators.** A family $(\rho_\varepsilon)_{\varepsilon>0}$ of probability measures on gauge fields on $M_R$ is *admissible* if it is (i) gauge invariant, (ii) reflection positive with respect to $\Theta$, (iii) local at range $O(\varepsilon)$, (iv) built from the metric $g_R$ alone, (v) CP invariant (so $\theta=0$). Two examples: the Wilson action on a cell complex of $S^3_R$ of mesh $\le\varepsilon$ times $\varepsilon\mathbb Z$ in time, with plaquette weights from $g_R$; and the gauge-covariant spectral (heat-trace) regularization of line 14 at $\Lambda_{\rm cut}=1/\varepsilon$.

*Reading of line 16's "spectral cutoff".* Line 14 found that a sharp cutoff in the Peter-Weyl sector index is not gauge invariant (flat-point value $-8.67$ against the gauge-invariant $-2$). So I read "the sphere spectral cutoff" in H2 and H5 as a gauge-covariant spectral regularization, not a sharp truncation in $j$. H2 and H5(a) must refer to the same family.

**Scheme and $\Lambda$.** A sphere scheme $S$ is a running coupling $g_S^2(R)$ defined from a dimensionless physical quantity of the sphere theory (for example a slow-room level spacing times $R$), normalized so that $g_S^2 = g_0^2 + O(g_0^4)$ at the cutoff. Its scale is
$$\Lambda_S = \lim_{R\to0}\ \frac1R\Big(\frac{\beta_0 g_S^2(R)}{16\pi^2}\Big)^{-\beta_1/(2\beta_0^2)}\exp\Big(-\frac{8\pi^2}{\beta_0 g_S^2(R)}\Big).$$
$\Lambda_{\rm lat}$ is defined the same way from the bare Wilson coupling $g_0(a)$ of line 1, with $a$ in place of $R$.

*Reading of line 20's $\Lambda$.* $\Lambda$ must be an $R$-independent constant. The one-loop formula on the page cannot both define $\Lambda$ and be the scheme coupling's exact running: the two-loop prefactor $(\beta_0 g^2/16\pi^2)^{-\beta_1/(2\beta_0^2)}$ diverges as $g\to0$. So I read line 20 as follows. $\Lambda := \Lambda_S$ is fixed, and $g^2(R) := 8\pi^2/(b_0\ln(1/(R\Lambda)))$ is a parametrization of $R$. Then $g_S^2(R) = g^2(R)\,[1+O(g^2\ln(1/g^2))]$ as $R\to0$, which is harmless for lines 12-19 because they hold for all small $g^2$.

**Local charts.** Fix $p_0\in S^3$ and an $h_t$-orthonormal frame $e$ at $p_0$. Let $\exp_{p_0}$ be the exponential map of $R^2h_t$, a diffeomorphism from the ball of radius $R\,\mathrm{inj}(h_t)$ onto its image ($\mathrm{inj}(h_1)=\pi$). Define
$$\varphi_R:\ B_{\rho R}\times\mathbb R\to M_R,\quad (x,\tau)\mapsto(\exp_{p_0}(x^ie_i),\tau),\qquad 0<\rho<\mathrm{inj}(h_t).$$
In normal coordinates $(\varphi_R^*g_R)_{ij} = \delta_{ij} - \tfrac13 \mathrm{Rm}_{ikjl}x^kx^l + O(|x|^3/R^3)$, with $|\mathrm{Rm}| = O(R^{-2})$. Since $\varphi_R$ is a product chart, it intertwines time translations and $\Theta$ exactly. Pushforward $\varphi_{R*}$ acts on $\mathcal O(\mathbb R^4)$ by moving loops, and by moving test functions with the flow taken in $g_R$.

### 1.2 The hypothesis

**H5(a) Existence on the sphere.** For every $R>0$ and every admissible family there is a bare coupling $g_0(\varepsilon)\to0$ (fixed by holding $g_S(R)$ at a prescribed value) such that, for all $n$ and $O_1,\dots,O_n\in\mathcal O(M_R)$,
$$S_R(O_1,\dots,O_n) := \lim_{\varepsilon\to0} \langle O_1\cdots O_n\rangle_{\rho_\varepsilon}$$
exists. The limit is the same for every admissible family once each is matched at the same $g_S(R)$. It satisfies:
- reflection positivity with respect to $\Theta$;
- invariance under the isometries of $h_t$ and under time translations;
- bounds $|S_R(O_1,\dots,O_n)|\le\prod_iC(O_i)$, where $C(O)$ depends on loop lengths, flow times and $\|f\|_{C^k}$.

OS reconstruction on $M_R$ then gives $(\mathcal H_R,H_R\ge0,\Omega_R)$. Moreover $\ker H_R=\mathbb C\,\Omega_R$.

**H5(b) Local flat limit.** Hold $\Lambda=\Lambda_S$ fixed and let $g_S(R)$ run. Then for all $O_i\in\mathcal O(\mathbb R^4)$ with compact support,
$$\lim_{R\to\infty}S_R(\varphi_{R*}O_1,\dots,\varphi_{R*}O_n) = S_\infty(O_1,\dots,O_n),$$
where $S_\infty$ is the $\mathbb R^4$ theory of line 23 at lattice scale $\Lambda_{\rm lat}=\Lambda_S/r_S(t)$. The limit does not depend on $p_0$, $e$ or $t$. For each fixed $O_1,\dots,O_n$ there is also the uniform bound $\sup_{R\ge R_0}|S_R(\varphi_{R*}O_1,\dots,\varphi_{R*}O_n)|<\infty$. Uniformity is over $R$, not over the observables. The expected rate is $O(R^{-2})$.

**H5(c) One renormalized theory.**
- (c1) Regulator independence on the sphere, as stated inside (a).
- (c2) $\Lambda_S(t) = r_S(t)\,\Lambda_{\rm lat}$ with $0<r_S(t)<\infty$. Here $r_S(t) = \exp(-8\pi^2 d_S(t)/\beta_0)$, and $d_S(t)$ is the one-loop matching constant in
$$g_S^{-2}(R) = g_0^{-2}(a) - \frac{\beta_0}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac Ra + d_S(t) + O(g_0^2).$$
This is exact, not only one-loop, because $\beta_0,\beta_1$ are scheme independent.
- (c3) The iterated limits ($\varepsilon\to0$, then $R\to\infty$) on the sphere and ($a\to0$ at fixed $L$, then $L\to\infty$) in the lattice box give the same $S_\infty$ when the scales are related by (c2).

**Order of limits.** $T\to\infty$ first (trivial at fixed $(R,\varepsilon)$, see step 1), then $\varepsilon\to0$ at fixed $R$, then $R\to\infty$. No joint limit is asserted.

## 2. Interface

**Line 16 (H2) → H5.** H2 bounds $\|P_{\rm low}(H_{\Lambda_{\rm cut}}-H_{\rm eff})P_{\rm low}\|$ uniformly in $\Lambda_{\rm cut}$. H5(a) supplies the limit $H_R$ itself. Combining them gives the same bound for $H_R$, provided $P_{\rm low}$ converges (strong resolvent convergence of the renormalized $H_{R,\Lambda_{\rm cut}}-E_{\rm vac}$ on the low-energy range). Both must use the same gauge-covariant regulator.

**Line 20 (H3) takes from H5:**
- (a): $H_R$ exists, so $\Delta(R) := \inf(\operatorname{spec}H_R\setminus\{0\})$ makes sense, and the vacuum is simple.
- (c2): the meaning of $\Lambda$. A bound $\Delta(R)\ge c\Lambda_S$ is the same as $\Delta(R)\ge c\,r_S\Lambda_{\rm lat}$.

*Line 20 needs more than H5 gives.* Its sketch joins a lattice end (lines 3-5, strong coupling at finite $a$) to the sphere family. H5 identifies continuum limits only. It says nothing that connects a strong-coupling lattice at finite spacing to the large-$R$ continuum sphere. That join is H3's own content.

**Line 23 (H4) takes from H5:** (b) and (c3), that is, a second construction of the $\mathbb R^4$ theory that agrees with the lattice route. Independence of the limit from $p_0$ and $e$ gives spatial translation and rotation invariance. On the round sphere this follows from the isometries already; for a Berger $t\ne1$ the isotropy is only $U(1)$, so frame independence is real content. Time translations and $\Theta$ come through exactly.

*What H5 does not give line 23.* $S^3\times\mathbb R$ has no isometry that mixes $\tau$ with space. The sphere route delivers $E(3)\times\mathbb R$, not $O(4)$. Full Euclidean symmetry has to come from H4's lattice side (restoration from the hypercubic group) or from a separate argument.

**Line 28 takes from H5:**
1. At each $R$: reflection positivity and reconstruction (a), and a simple vacuum (a). The identity $\langle A;e^{-\tau H_R}A\rangle\le\|(A-\langle A\rangle)\Omega_R\|^2e^{-\tau\Delta(R)}$ fails if $\ker H_R$ is degenerate. Line 28 cites the lattice positivity-improving transfer matrix [PC]. In the continuum sphere theory, "improving" does not survive limits (only "preserving" does), so simplicity is a separate requirement on H5(a).
2. A set $D\subset\mathcal O(\mathbb R^4)$ of observables supported at $\tau>0$ whose vectors are dense in $\mathcal H_\infty$, such that one- and two-point functions converge pointwise in $\tau$ by (b), and $\sup_R\|(A-\langle A\rangle_R)\Omega_R\|^2<\infty$ by (b)'s uniform bound.
3. That the $\Lambda$ in line 20 is the $\mathbb R^4$ theory's own scale, up to $r_S$, by (c2) and (c3).

Then for every fixed $\tau$,
$$S^T_\infty(\Theta A,A_\tau) = \lim_R S^T_R(\Theta\varphi_{R*}A,\varphi_{R*}A_\tau)\le\sup_R\|(A-\langle A\rangle_R)\Omega_R\|^2\,e^{-\tau c\Lambda_S},$$
and the lemma of line 28 gives $\operatorname{spec}H\cap(0,c\,r_S\Lambda_{\rm lat})=\emptyset$. Because $\varphi_R$ is a product chart, $\Theta$ and time translation on $M_R$ match those on $\mathbb R^4$, which is why this works.

**What H5 takes from others:** line 1 (action), line 2 (reflection positivity, for the cell-complex regulator), line 14 (one-loop universality), line 21/23 (the box route), and line 20 if the decay estimate of step 6 is used. *No circularity:* step 6 must use line 20's sphere-side gap, never line 28's $\mathbb R^4$ gap or line 27's uniqueness, both of which depend on H5.

## 3. What is known

**Proven:**
- Reflection positivity of lattice gauge theory and the transfer matrix (Osterwalder and Seiler 1978, Ann. Phys.). The proof uses only the time-reflection structure, so I expect it to carry over verbatim to (cell complex of $S^3$)$\times\varepsilon\mathbb Z$. That is a check, not a citation.
- Ultraviolet stability of 4D lattice Yang-Mills in a finite periodic box (Bałaban, Commun. Math. Phys. 1987-89). Construction of SU(2) $YM_4$ Schwinger functions with the UV cutoff removed and an IR cutoff kept, trivial sector (Magnen, Rivasseau and Sénéor 1993, Commun. Math. Phys. 155). Neither treats curved space or removes the IR cutoff.
- One-loop $\Lambda$ ratios between lattice and continuum schemes are finite and computable (Hasenfratz and Hasenfratz 1980; Dashen and Gross 1981). Two-loop universality of $\beta_1$ (Caswell 1974; Jones 1974).
- The one-loop divergences of gauge fields on a curved 4-manifold are local: Seeley-DeWitt $a_2$ (DeWitt 1965; Gilkey 1975). The $\operatorname{tr}F^2$ coefficient is metric independent, and the other terms are c-numbers in the curvature.
- In 2D, Yang-Mills on any compact surface is constructed exactly, and its holonomy field is local and Markovian (Driver 1989; Sengupta 1997; Lévy 2003, 2010). So the 2D analogue of H5(b) (sphere of growing area, plane limit on compact sets) follows from the heat-kernel structure. Line 6 is in this regime.
- Small-volume perturbation theory: on the torus (Lüscher 1983, Nucl. Phys. B219), where zero modes give expansions in $g^{2/3}$; on $S^3$ the effective Hamiltonian of van Baal and van den Heuvel (1990s).

**Computed here (line 14):** on $S^3_t$, the log-divergent coefficient of $\tfrac12\sum_{\rm fast}\sqrt{\mu_n}$ equals $(b_0/8\pi^2)S_{\rm cl}$ to $10^{-4}$ along the Maurer-Cartan line, and to $0.5\%$ in curvature at the background ($t=3,s=1.15$). This is one-loop universality between the sphere and flat space. The finite part $E_0^{\rm fin}$, which fixes $d_S(t)$, is computed in the $\zeta$ scheme at $\mu R=1$, with scheme freedom $E_0\to E_0+\kappa\,dC$.

**Expected, not proved:** everything in H5. As far as I know, no construction of 4D Yang-Mills on $S^3\times\mathbb R$ exists. Universality between regularizations of 4D Yang-Mills is unproved beyond perturbation theory.

## 4. Best-guess proof

**Step 1 (regulator, finite level).** Cubulate $S^3$ as the boundary of the 4-cube (8 cubes), subdivide each cube $N^3$ times, project radially onto $(S^3,R^2h_t)$, and use the Wilson action with metric weights on (complex)$\times\varepsilon\mathbb Z$.
- *Claim:* this is reflection positive with respect to $\Theta$ (Osterwalder-Seiler argument).
- *Claim:* at fixed $(R,\varepsilon)$ the spatial configuration space $G^{\#\text{edges}}$ is compact, and the transfer matrix is compact and positivity improving. Hence it has a simple top eigenvalue and a gap, so $T\to\infty$ is trivial. (Standard; the same as for a finite lattice.)

**Step 2 (power counting with curvature and defects).** The cubulation has fixed conical defects along the tesseract's 1-skeleton (three cubes around an edge, deficit $\pi/2$ before projection). In spacetime these defects are 2-dimensional sheets.
- *Claim:* there are no relevant or marginal counterterms except $g_0^{-2}$ and c-numbers.
- Bulk: the gauge-invariant local scalars of dimension $\le4$ are $1$, $\operatorname{tr}F^2$ and $\operatorname{tr}F\tilde F$. The last is excluded by CP, and pure Yang-Mills has no gauge-invariant dimension-2 scalar, so there is no $\mathcal R\cdot(\dim 2)$ term. The curvature-only terms $\mathcal R$, $\mathcal R^2$, Ric$^2$ and Riem$^2$ cancel in normalized correlators.
- Defects: a defect operator on a 2D sheet needs dimension $\le2$, and only the identity qualifies.
- This is perturbative power counting. Its non-perturbative validity is [guess].

**Step 3 (UV limit at fixed $R$; H5(a)).** [guess] Run a Bałaban-type block-spin RG on the sphere. Its small-field/large-field decomposition is local, so curvature enters only through block geometry at relative size $(L^k\varepsilon/R)^2$. The key estimate is, for $L^k\varepsilon\le R$,
$$S^{(k)} = g_k^{-2}S_W^{(k)} + c_k + E^{(k)},\qquad \|E^{(k)}\|_{k}\le C\Big(g_k^{2} + \big(L^k\varepsilon/R\big)^2\Big),$$
uniformly in $\varepsilon$, with $g_k$ following the two-loop flow. The flow stops when the block scale reaches $R$. What remains is a finite-dimensional gauge integral over block variables on $S^3_R$ at coupling $g_S(R)$: the slow room of lines 7-9 plus finitely many fast sectors.
- *The point of using my programme here:* the small room is where Bałaban's flow ends on the sphere.
- For small $R$, $g_S(R)$ is small. Theorem A (line 9, $\kappa>0$) gives the slow modes a harmonic part, so the expansion is in $g^2$, unlike the torus's $g^{2/3}$, and the endpoint integral is controlled.
- For large $R$ the endpoint is strongly coupled. Then H5(a) still holds (finitely many compact variables), but without quantitative control.
- *Simple vacuum:* at small $R$, from H2 together with Perron-Frobenius on the reduced space (line 27's [PC] in the reduction). For general $R$ I take it from line 20's meaning of $\Delta(R)$, as a gap above a simple vacuum [guess].

**Step 4 (regulator independence; (c1)).** [guess] Two flows meet. Run both admissible regulators down to a common block scale $a_*$ with $\varepsilon\ll a_*\ll R$. By step 3 both effective actions have the form $g_*^{-2}S_W+c+E$, with $\|E\|\le C(g_*^2+(a_*/R)^2)$. Matching $g_*$ (one parameter, by step 2) and using Lipschitz continuity of the remaining integration in the effective action gives
$$|S^{(1)}_R(O)-S^{(2)}_R(O)|\le C_O\big(g_*^2+(a_*/R)^2\big)\xrightarrow{a_*\to0}0.$$

**Step 5 (the $\Lambda$ ratio; (c2)).** Write $g_X^{-2}(\mu) = (\beta_0/8\pi^2)\ln(\mu/\Lambda_X)+\beta_1(16\pi^2\beta_0)^{-1}\ln\ln(\mu^2/\Lambda_X^2)+o(1)$. The two-loop term has the same coefficient in both schemes, and its difference between the two scales vanishes as both couplings go to zero. Subtracting at $\mu_{\rm lat}=1/a$, $\mu_S=1/R$ gives
$$g_S^{-2}(R)-g_0^{-2}(a) = -\frac{\beta_0}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac Ra+\frac{\beta_0}{8\pi^2}\ln\frac{\Lambda_{\rm lat}}{\Lambda_S}+o(1),$$
so $d_S(t)=(\beta_0/8\pi^2)\ln(\Lambda_{\rm lat}/\Lambda_S)$ and $r_S=e^{-8\pi^2d_S/\beta_0}$. It is finite because the logarithms cancel, and line 14 checks exactly that: the sphere log coefficient is $b_0$, independent of $t$, the same as the lattice one. $d_S(t)$ is the difference between the finite part of $E_0^{\rm fin}$ along $S_{\rm cl}$ and the flat-lattice one-loop constant. I have not computed it.
- *Consistency with line 15:* the scheme shift $\kappa$ is exactly this freedom. Line 15 shows the Helike-competitor order survives until $\kappa\approx15.8$ at $g^2=0.002$, which is a scale change of $e^{16}$.

**Step 6 (local flat limit; (b)).** Two parts.
- *(6i) Compactness:* the local bounds of step 3 on blocks smaller than $\rho R$ are uniform in $R$, and give tightness of the pulled-back Schwinger functions [guess]. This is the IR problem, just as for $L\to\infty$ on the torus.
- *(6ii) Identification:* interpolate the metric on $B_{\rho R}$ between $\varphi_R^*g_R$ and $\delta$. The derivative is a stress-tensor insertion, finite under the gradient-flow definition (Suzuki 2013), which uses H6. With $\delta g_{ij}(x)=O(|x|^2/R^2)$ and decay rate $m>0$ of connected correlators,
$$\Big|S_R(\varphi_{R*}O)-S_\infty(O)\Big|\ \le\ \frac{C_O}{R^2}\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^2e^{-m\,\mathrm{dist}(x,K)}\,d^4x\ +\ C'_O\,e^{-m\rho R}\ =\ O(R^{-2}).$$
- The first term is the curvature correction. The second is the global closing of the sphere, which needs decay of boundary influence (a mixing condition), not just truncated decay.
- Decay in $\tau$ comes from line 20. Decay in space uniform in $R$ is a sub-conjecture **[SC]**. On $S^3\times\mathbb R$ it does not follow from the $\tau$-gap, because there is no rotation mixing $\tau$ and space.
- Evidence for SC: the strong-coupling cluster expansion (line 3) gives spatial and temporal decay together, and lattice glueball data in large boxes show finite correlation lengths in every direction.
- Independence of $p_0$, $e$ and $t$ follows because the right-hand side does not depend on them.

**Step 7 ((c3)).** Steps 4 and 5 make both families flow, at short distance, to $g^{-2}S_W$ with $\Lambda$ matched. Step 6 and line 23 send both to $\mathbb R^4$. If the $\mathbb R^4$ limit with given $\Lambda$, $\theta=0$ and E(3) invariance is unique, the two limits agree [guess; uniqueness is the real content].

## 5. Where it can fail

- **A non-covariant sphere regulator.** Extra counterterms ($\operatorname{tr}A^2$, $\mathcal R\operatorname{tr}A^2$) would appear and the limit would depend on the regulator. Observed signature: the gauge-equivalence test fails, as the sector-index $\zeta$ already did ($-8.67$ against $-2$).
- **A $\theta$ mismatch.** If one side breaks CP, $\theta\ne0$ is generated, and $\operatorname{tr}F\tilde F$ is exactly marginal. Observed: $\langle Q\rangle\ne0$ or CP-odd correlators.
- **Coupling to non-metric structure.** $O(1/R)$ instead of $O(1/R^2)$ corrections would signal coupling to a structure beyond the metric, such as the Hopf direction of a Berger-adapted complex. Observed: convergence rate $R^{-1}$, or dependence of the limit on $t$ or on the frame.
- **Spatial clustering fails uniformly in $R$ (SC false),** or a transition occurs in $R$ (a non-analyticity in the full theory, not the exact crossing of line 17). Observed: dependence on $p_0$, or $S_R$ failing to converge. Finite-temperature transitions on $S^3\times S^1$ are avoided because time is $\mathbb R$.
- **Non-uniqueness of the $\mathbb R^4$ limit** (several subsequential limits). Observed: the limit depends on $t$, or the sphere and box limits differ at matched $\Lambda$.
- **The sphere scheme is ill-defined at two loops** (non-analytic slow-room dependence). The harmonic slow modes of line 9 make this unlikely.

## 6. Smallest next step

**One-loop two-regulator equivalence on $S^3_t\times\mathbb R$.** Let $\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rm W,\varepsilon}[A]$ be the one-loop background-field effective action of the metric-weighted Wilson action on the refined tesseract complex, and $\Gamma^{(1)}_{\zeta,\Lambda_{\rm cut}}[A]$ that of line 14's heat-trace regularization. Then, uniformly for backgrounds $A$ in a $C^k$-bounded set,
$$\Gamma^{(1)}_{\rm W,\varepsilon}[A]-\Gamma^{(1)}_{\zeta,1/\varepsilon}[A] = d(t)\,S_{\rm cl}[A] + c(\varepsilon,R,t) + o(1)\quad(\varepsilon\to0),$$
with $d(t)$ finite and independent of $A$, and $c$ a c-number. In particular the conical defects contribute only to $c$.

This proves (c2) at the level that is exact for $\Lambda$ ratios. It also tests step 2's defect power counting, and it is the first rung of step 4. It rests directly on line 14.

None. The Jaffe-Witten statement asks for existence and a mass gap on $\mathbb R^4$ ("for any compact simple gauge group G, a non-trivial quantum Yang-Mills theory exists on $\mathbb R^4$ and has a mass gap $\Delta>0$"). H5 is a route to that, not a requirement of it. The one interface gap, that $S^3\times\mathbb R$ cannot supply $O(4)$ invariance, falls under Euclidean symmetry, which line 23 (H4) already requires.
