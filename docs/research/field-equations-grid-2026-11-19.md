**Tags** (my convention):

- **STRUCTURAL:** the same mathematical object, in a different setting.
- **RESONANCE:** the same shape, on a different object.

## What the grid forces (sudoku consequences)

If the trichotomy's intuition is right in the gauge setting, as the count suggests, then three things
follow. Each is testable.

1. **Every exactly-determined reading is critical.** Any recovery whose clues equal its unknowns, at any
   order or in any group, should be unstable in the same way. That includes a covariant SU(2) hybrid read
   on too few rings.
   - **Test:** the SU(2) second-order reading on two rings. The prediction is amplification of order 10³,
     as in the Abelian case.
2. **The stable margin is a ratio, not a count.** The trichotomy puts the boundary at Γ = 1, with
   stability growing with Γ. The measured votes (about 2.4 for two rings linear, about 1.9 for three rings
   second order) should fix how much margin a reading needs.
   - **Prediction:** a reading at Γ about 1.3 (for example, a partial third ring, as in the ring-fold
     experiment) is stable but with amplification well above the two-ring value.
   - The ring-fold result (two to four selected second-ring cells per cell passes BW05 and S3) is
     consistent with this. It is not yet tested against Γ.
3. **The landscape around a non-stationary holonomy energy has a computable floor.** E5 says completions
   flow towards stationary holonomy. The Floor Theorem says how steep the walls are on the way there.
   - **Consequence for the variational force:** a seated, seamless reading (M0) inherits a floor from the
     same operator. Its wobble count is the number of directions where the floor is lost. P-wobble in M0
     measures exactly this (360 free modes unseated, 0 seated, at n = 2).
