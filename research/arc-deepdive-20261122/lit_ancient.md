## 0. Corrections to the arc note (read these first)

Fetching the sources made the Part III paragraph more accurate in five places. The notes on each are in the sections below.

1. **Her name's etymology runs through *helix*, not straight from *helissō*.** LSJ derives ἑλίκη from ἕλιξ ("winding")
   and glosses the Great Bear "from its revolving round the pole". Frisk treats ἑλίσσω as a verb *derived from* ἕλιξ, and
   the family as probably Pre-Greek. So "same family as *helix* and *helissō*, named for its turning" is right, but "from
   *helissō*" (the-twist-a-note-2026-11-21.md, line 52, and the arc) is slightly off. The "willow" sense of ἑλίκη is a
   separate word (§3).
2. **Aratus says "smaller orbit", not "nearer the pole".** The Greek is μειοτέρῃ … στροφάλιγγι, "a smaller whirl"
   (Phaen. 43). Being nearer the pole is the astronomical reason for the smaller orbit, but Aratus does not say it.
   "Crossed the sea more truly" does match l. 44: ἰθύντατα ναυτίλλονται, "steer the straightest course".
3. **Plato's "X" is in the Greek, not in Lamb's English.** Burnet's Greek has οἷον χεῖ, "like a chi". Lamb translates it
   as "like a great cross", and LSJ cites this very passage for χεῖ as "name of the letter χ".
4. **Fuxi and Nüwa: the Astana paintings are Tang, not Han.** The Astana silk painting in the Xinjiang museum is Tang
   (618–907). The Han examples are the Wu family shrine reliefs (rubbing dated 147). In both, **Fuxi holds the square
   and Nüwa the compass**, and their tails are intertwined. No source I fetched uses "mirror image".
5. **"Pythagoras's monochord" is the later legend.** The Stanford Encyclopedia calls the discovery stories "clearly
   false". The ratio knowledge is probably early Pythagorean, and the working experiment is credited to Hippasus. The
   monochord was called the *kanôn*. Better wording: "the Pythagorean monochord".

One finding goes the other way and makes the resonance stronger than I had it: **Clifford himself called the motions
whose invariant lines are his parallels "twists", right- and left-handed** (§6).

- **PROVED (elementary).** Two distinct great circles on a 2-sphere meet in exactly two antipodal points. Two planes
  through the centre meet in a line, and the line meets the sphere twice. That is Plato's construction: the strips
  cross at the middle (36b) and join again "at a point opposite" (36c, ἐν τῷ καταντικρὺ).
- **IMAGINED.** The arc says "two hands, joined at a crossing". The text gives: one circle kept whole (the Same), one

  more than the likeness. Plato's two circles lie on one 2-sphere and must cross. Clifford's and Hopf's circles in S³
  never meet, but every pair is linked once.

spiral (Il.) … Derivatives: ἑλίκη 1. willow s. v.; 2. spiral, turning (Arist.), also name of the Great Bear (because of

## 6. Clifford 1873: the parallels, and the word "twist"

**QUOTED (bibliographic, secondary).** "[Clifford 1873] Clifford, W. K., Preliminary Sketch of Biquaternions,
Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, 4, 64-65, 381-395, 1873. Also in [Clifford Mathematical Papers,
181-200]." And: "What nowadays is known as Clifford's parallelism in 3–dimensional elliptic space was first sketched by
Clifford in a short memoir [Clifford 1873] that was presented at the London Mathematical Society in June 1873."
Source: "Variations on a theme: Clifford's parallelism in elliptic space", University of Pisa repository.
URL: https://arpi.unipi.it/retrieve/e0d6c930-965a-fcf8-e053-d805fe0aa794/Cliffordrevised23.pdf
(**UNVERIFIED author.** The PDF has no byline. A PhilPapers record id beginning "COG" suggests Cogliati, but I could
not open it.)

**QUOTED (Clifford's own words, through the same secondary source).** Clifford 1873, p. 390: "we may define a vector
as a motor whose axes are indeterminate; and the case we are now considering is the only case of such indetermination
which occurs in elliptic geometry. Vectors will be called right or left according as the twist of them is right– or
left–handed". The Pisa author adds: "Clifford stated the proposition according to which every motor in elliptic space is
the sum of a right and a left vector."

**QUOTED (M. Epple, ICM 2002, arXiv:math/0305023).** "any given quaternion different from zero induces two isometries
of elliptic space by left and right multiplication. Such isometries Clifford called left and right 'twists',
respectively. (Felix Klein would later term them 'Schiebungen', translations.) Every twist possessed a space-filling
family of invariant lines … Any two members of one and the same such family were called 'parallels' by Clifford." Also,
on the flat torus: "a finite parallelogram whose opposite sides … are regarded as identical" [Clifford, Math. Papers
p. 193].
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305023

- **UNVERIFIED (primary).** The Zenodo scan of the 1873 paper returned "403 Forbidden", so I have not read Clifford
  myself. The words above are Clifford's as quoted by the Pisa paper, and Epple's summary.
- **IMAGINED, and the strongest echo in this thread.** I named her "the twist" for what she does around the long
  circle. Clifford named the motions whose orbits *are* the long circles "twists", and sorted them into right- and
  left-handed: two hands. Every motion splits into a right part and a left part. This is a fact about the stage
  (left and right multiplication on S³ = SU(2)), not about her. But it is the same vocabulary for the same object,
  150 years earlier.

## 7. Hopf 1931: the fibres are Clifford's circles, and every two are linked once

**QUOTED (primary; GDZ scan of Math. Ann. 104 (1931) 637–665, read page by page).**
URL: https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/hopf.pdf (bibliographic record:
https://eudml.org/doc/159489, DOI 10.1007/BF01457962)
- p. 637, Satz I: "Die Abbildungen der 3-dimensionalen Sphäre S³ auf die 2-dimensionale Sphäre S² bilden unendlich
  viele Klassen."
- p. 638: "γ ist die Verschlingungszahl der Originalzyklen zweier beliebiger Punkte x und y."
- p. 655, eq. (4): "z = (x₁ + i x₂)/(x₃ + i x₄)". The fibre of each point is "ein Großkreis".
- p. 655: "je zwei zueinander fremde Großkreise der S³ sind miteinander verschlungen, und zwar ist ihre
  Verschlingungszahl ±1."
- p. 655, footnote 13: "Das System dieser Großkreise, die die Originalmengen der Punkte von S² bilden, ist eine
  Cliffordsche Parallelenkongruenz; hierzu vgl. man F. Klein, Vorlesungen über Nichteuklidische Geometrie (Berlin 1928),
  S. 234".

**QUOTED (Epple, as above).** "Clifford's parallels provided him with the crucial example of a mapping from S³ to S² that
was not homotopic to a constant mapping … Even Hopf's basic invariant for classifying such maps, the linking number of
fibres, was derived from an intuitive understanding that the linking behaviour of Clifford's parallels was an obstacle
for deforming the Hopf fibration into a constant".

**COMPUTED.** `hopf1931_linking_check.py` (receipt `hopf1931_linking_check.receipt.txt`). For 6 random pairs of points
z₁, z₂ the script does the following:
- It builds Hopf's fibres from eq. (4) and confirms each is a great circle (rank 2, with z constant to 1e-15).
- It confirms that the distance from one fibre to the other is constant (spread ≤ 4e-15), so the fibres are Clifford
  parallel.
- It computes the Gauss linking integral after a generic stereographic projection: −1.0000 in all six cases. That is
  Hopf's ±1, with the sign set by orientation.

- **UNVERIFIED.** I did not fetch Klein 1928, p. 234.
- **IMAGINED.** Each long circle of my Berger sphere is one of these fibres. Plato's two circles must cross. Hopf's two
  circles can never cross and can never be pulled apart. "Joined at a crossing" (the arc) may be the wrong image for
  her and her twin; "linked once, never touching" is the one the stage actually provides.

## 8. Hamilton 1843: the quaternions, and the stone on the bridge

**QUOTED (primary; Hamilton to Rev. Archibald H. Hamilton, letter dated August 5, 1865, transcribed by D. R. Wilkins,
TCD).** "But on the 16th day of the same month - which happened to be a Monday, and a Council day of the Royal Irish
Academy - I was walking in to attend and preside, and your mother was walking with me, along the Royal Canal … An
electric circuit seemed to close; and a spark flashed forth … Nor could I resist the impulse - unphilosophical as it may
have been - to cut with a knife on a stone of Brougham Bridge, as we passed it, the fundamental formula with the
symbols, i, j, k; namely, i² = j² = k² = ijk = -1".
Also: "your (then) little brother William Edwin, and yourself, used to ask me, 'Well, Papa, can you multiply triplets'?
Whereto I was always obliged to reply, with a sad shake of the head: 'No, I can only add and subtract them.'"
URL: https://www.maths.tcd.ie/pub/HistMath/People/Hamilton/Letters/BroomeBridge.html

**QUOTED (same page, Hamilton to P. G. Tait, October 15, 1858).** "They started into life, or light, full grown, on
[Monday] the 16th of October, 1843, as I was walking with Lady Hamilton to Dublin, and came up to Brougham Bridge, which
my boys have since called the Quaternion Bridge."

**QUOTED (primary; Hamilton to J. T. Graves, October 17, 1843, published in Phil. Mag. xxv (1844) 489–95).** "there
dawned on me the notion that we must admit, in some sense, a fourth dimension of space for the purpose of calculating
with triplets; or transferring the paradox to algebra, must admit a third distinct imaginary symbol k, not to be
confounded with either i or j, but equal to the product of the first as multiplier, and the second as multiplicand".
URL: https://www.maths.tcd.ie/pub/HistMath/People/Hamilton/QLetter/QLetter.html

- **PROVED (textbook).** The unit quaternions |q| = 1 form the 3-sphere in ℝ⁴, a group (SU(2)). Left and right
  multiplication by unit quaternions are Clifford's left and right twists (§6). Hopf's z = (x₁+ix₂)/(x₃+ix₄) is the
  quotient by one of those circle actions (§7). That is the line Hamilton → Clifford → Hopf in one sentence.
- **IMAGINED.** On this "imaginary arc", Hamilton's step was a third imaginary, "not to be confounded with either i
  or j". The stage she lives on came from an extra imaginary direction.

## Suggested replacement text for arc Part III (for my review)

> pole" (LSJ); it is of the family of *helix* and *helissō*, to turn. **Which circle to steer by.** In Aratus
