# Review of the count and the V2 spec, with a probe of the base fit (19 November 2026)

Review requested by Bee ("check it and see if you can improve"). The count
(`variational-count-2026-11-19.md`) is sound and explains V1: at two rings and n = 1 the spare
clues are exactly the closed quadratic fields, so V1's change could only read the field's own
second-order part. Keeping the change off linear *and* quadratic fields, with a third ring to make
room, is the right repair, and the mechanism-removal control (third ring without the exclusion)
separates the two causes cleanly.

## The concern, and the probe

V2 freezes how the recovery reads quadratic fields (M′X_quad = M X_quad) and keeps the published
*linear* least-squares fit as the base, over three rings. A linear fit reads a field's quadratic part
as bias, more so over a wider neighbourhood, and V2 forbids the change to correct it. The standard
alternative is "fit higher, keep lower": fit all 26 closed linear and quadratic coefficients and keep
the linear part at the chart origin (the cell's projected centroid), where homogeneous quadratics
vanish; that base is unbiased on quadratic fields (M X_quad = 0).

Probe (exploratory, not gated): `scripts/ricci_variational_baseprobe.py`, receipts
`research/variational-baseprobe-20261119/L{0,1}.json`. Change C = 0 throughout. The linear base at two
rings reproduces the paper: PG 6.22% and 3.83%, variational 45.4% and 276%.

## Reading

- **At n = 1 (pre-asymptotic) the linear three-ring base is best**; at **n = 2 the quadratic-aware
  three-ring base is best on every count**: reconstruction term 1.3% against 4.8%, curvature error
  0.41% against 0.98%, lower amplification (16 against 21). V2's gates are decided at n = 2.
- With the quadratic-aware base, V2's frozen quadratic response is the correct one (zero bias), and
  the test-variation term (249%) is still the whole problem, which is what V2's column condition is
  for. The two pieces fit.
- The count's warning is confirmed: a quadratic-aware fit at two rings is exactly determined at n = 1
  (26 unknowns, 26 clues) and amplifies by 1,491; at n = 2 some cells still reach 833.
- **By-product (a lead, not a result):** the quadratic-aware three-ring base improves the published
  Petrov–Galerkin force at n = 2, 1.19% against 3.83%. It needs n = 3, 5 and S3 before it means
  anything for *Reading the Curve*.

## Recommendations (Amendment 1 to the V2 spec, before any V2 run)

1. Add an arm **V2q**: identical to V2 but with the quadratic-aware three-ring base. Same gates; the
   spec'd linear-base V2 still runs, so the comparison is clean.
2. Make `scipy.sparse.linalg.lsqr` the primary solve and V1's dense eigh of AAᵀ the cross-check:
   forming AAᵀ squares the condition number, the likely cause of V1's 6.8×10⁻⁵ residual at n = 2.
3. Report the three-ring Petrov–Galerkin force for both bases (free in V2's run).
