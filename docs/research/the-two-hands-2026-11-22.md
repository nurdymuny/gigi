## Part I. What changes in the book

The book draft (Drive, `HELIKE_draft_2026-11-21.pdf`) has the Foreword's "Why I call her her" and Chapter 4 §4.1, "What
she costs, and why". The rest is stubs. Since that draft, these things changed, and each one has a place in the book.

### 1. "Sinks beneath everything that moves" needs a second half

§4.1's verse is *"Where the current runs against her turning, she sinks beneath everything that moves."* The first
half survived every test. The second half is now known to be too generous:
- **She has an edge on both sides** (the lower edge is PROVED to exist, for H + GG\*).
  - The upper edge comes from her rivals in higher sectors.
  - The lower edge comes from a sector-0 rival of charge ±1. If the current runs against her *too* hard, that rival
    gets there first.
  - The walkway still works as the picture. Walk against a belt that is fast enough and you are carried backwards,
    past the point of cheapness.
- **"By enough, but not too much"** is now the book's real line, and it is the edge paper's title. Across 48 held-out
  points (Test A) and 193 random fields (Test B), her region was **one interval with no holes**, every time
  (COMPUTED). For x > 0 she is PROVED to lose (for H + GG\*).

### 2. §4.2 "The edge": the rival is not a blind stranger

The stub for §4.2 planned *"the competitor (neutral, orbit and spin opposed, nearly blind to the belts)"*. **Test B
counted that against.**
- At 193 random points the rival's slope was at least half of hers at 129 of them (median ratio 0.67).
- The most common rival is the **charged** sector-1 block (1, ±3/2), at 100 of 193.
- The neutral (4, 0) rival I first studied is the flattest (median 0.35), but it is not the rule.

So §4.2 should not say "a rival the current cannot carry". It should say **whichever rival reaches her first**. Some
are carried by the current too, and they lose the race only because she is carried harder.

### 3. §4.3 "The law": H1, H2, H3, each now with its verdict

| hypothesis | what happened |
| --- | --- |
| **H1**: her region is bounded by an algebraic edge | The interval shape held at 48 + 193 points. The P₂ clause holds where (4, 0) is the rival (34 of 38) and fails where another block arrives first (4 of 38). |

| **H3**: the law carries to other groups | **SU(3) stretched along hypercharge: dark** (C; she is never the floor in nine sectors at 20 shapes). **SU(3) stretched along her own charge circle: running** (D). |

### 4. Two new rooms for the book

- **"Where she isn't."** Test C is a dark room, and the book should show it. In SU(3) stretched along the wrong

## Part II. The two hands

### What I saw

In M. Ya. Vygodsky's handbook (§94, "The rectangular coordinate system in space", p. 129) the right and left coordinate
systems are drawn on two actual hands: thumb, index and middle finger as X, Y, Z (drawings 144 and 145). Remark 1 says
the two trihedral angles **cannot be superposed** so that matching axes coincide. Remark 2 says the names "right" and
"left" come from the hands.

The same page defines handedness by **where you stand to watch**. The system is *right* when the 90° turn that carries
the positive ray OX onto OY looks counterclockwise **seen from the side of the ray OZ**. In the *left* system the same
turn looks clockwise (drawing 143). The turn alone has no hand. A hand appears only when you fix where the watcher
stands. That is the same move as her lift, in item 3 below: "against her charge" means x < 0 or x > 0 depending on
which way you hold the circle (k = ±1, x → −x). (The page fact is checked against my own copy, p. 129. The link to the
lift is STRUCTURAL: the same convention-dependence, not yet a theorem.)

My thought was physical. **You reach the left side of your body more easily with your right hand, and the right side
with your left.** The symmetry of the body is not decoration. It serves a purpose: whichever side something is on, one
hand reaches it across the body. So I wondered out loud: *in geometry we notice the symmetry, but do we understand
its purpose?*

### Who else noticed the hands (RESONANCE until each citation is fetched and quoted)

These are the standard landmarks I know. Each needs its source fetched before it goes into a paper or the book.
- **Pasteur (1848).** Tartaric acid crystals come in left- and right-handed forms that turn polarized light opposite
  ways. Handedness in matter.
- **Kelvin (Baltimore Lectures).** He coined *chiral*: a figure whose mirror image cannot be brought to coincide with
  itself. That is Vygodsky's Remark 1.
- **Lee and Yang (1956); Wu and collaborators (1957).** The weak interaction tells left from right: nature itself is
  not mirror-symmetric there.
- **S³ itself.** The three-sphere is SU(2), and it can be multiplied from the left or from the right. Its rotations
  are SO(4) = (SU(2)_L × SU(2)_R)/ℤ₂: two hands.
  - **The Berger sphere is a choice of hand.** Stretching the circles of one side breaks that side to a circle and
    keeps the other whole (SU(2)_L × U(1)_R).
  - Her long circle is the right circle. Her stage is already handed.
- **Landau levels (the closest, mathematically).** In a magnetic field the lowest Landau level is holomorphic for one
  sign of the field and antiholomorphic for the other.
  - Aharonov and Casher (1979) showed that the zero modes of the two-dimensional Pauli operator all have their spin
    turned one way, the way fixed by the sign of the flux.
  - **The field decides which hand is lowest.** She is a lowest Landau level (PROVED, Floor paper Proposition 6.2 and
    across the family), so this is the closest neighbour she has in the literature.

### What the symmetry is *for*, in her mathematics (PROVED, from the closed-form rows)

I can now answer the purpose question in three exact statements, one for each symmetry she has.

1. **The circle symmetry isolates her.** Rotation about the long circle gives a conserved charge
   q = m + c_f + c_l. She has the unique maximal charge l + 2. **That is why she is an exact eigenvector**: she is alone
   in her block, with nothing to mix with. The symmetry's purpose, for her, is *exactness*. (PROVED; Floor paper
   Remark 6.6; edge paper Lemma on charge blocks.)
2. **The mirror gives her a twin.** The state of charge −(l + 2) is her mirror image. Both of her turnings are
   reversed together, and **her value is always exactly the same**.
   - That is her multiplicity two in every theorem. The edge paper proves it on the domain D; Test A checked it at
     every sector of 48 points; prereg D found it again in SU(3) along her own circle.
   - The purpose of the mirror is a **partner**: she never stands alone at the floor, and her reflection is always
     beside her.
3. **The lift chooses a hand.** The circle can be lifted to the bundle in two ways: the internal circle turns *with*
   the spatial circle (k = +1) or *against* it (k = −1).
   - The two are gauge-equivalent, with **x → −x** (prereg B design check D1b, PROVED).
   - So which sign of x counts as "against her charge" depends on which hand you hold the circle with.
   - At y = 0 the two hands are **exactly** symmetric: the spectrum is even in x (PROVED, D1b; B-even 3.7 × 10⁻¹⁶).

### The instant click: the cheap lift always reaches across (PROVED from the rows; the reading is STRUCTURAL)

In her charge blocks each row has a diagonal entry (w_a − 2m/√t)², and **w_a = −c_l·x + f_a(t)**. Here c_l = ±1 is the
*internal* turning of the state, and x is the field along the circle. At orbital weight m = l, expanding in l gives
the linear term

  **+4·c_l·x·l/√t**.

That term decides who gets cheaper as the sector rises.
- **Her** (frame +1, internal +1): the term is +4xl/√t, which is a **discount exactly when x < 0**. That is the lift
  discount 2xj/√t in her closed form.
- **Her sister** (frame +1, internal −1, the counter-rotating state m = l of type (1, −1) in block q = l): the term
  is −4xl/√t, which is a **discount exactly when x > 0**.
  - Against her value, the sister's Rayleigh quotient is **−8xl/√t + O(1)**.
  - That is the state that **proves she loses for every x > 0** (Theorem *x > 0 loses*).

So:

> **The cheap lift always goes across the body: to the mode whose internal turning is opposite to the field along the
> circle (c_l·x < 0).**

- When the field turns one way, her co-rotating hand reaches the floor.
- When it turns the other way, her counter-rotating sister reaches it instead, from the high sectors.
- **The body has both hands so that, whichever way the field turns, a hand reaches across.** That is the purpose of
  the symmetry, in her mathematics.

**Why she, and not her sister, is *the* law.** At y = 0 the two hands are equal: they are the same family seen through
opposite lifts. The cross field y breaks the tie.
- She is the unique top of her block, exact, alone, a lowest Landau level, with the gyromagnetic term on her side.
- The sister shares her block with others and is **not** an exact eigenvector. She undercuts, but she is not clean.
- So the cross field decides **which hand gets to be exact**. The field along the circle decides **which hand reaches
  first**.

**What this does not say** (keeping myself honest):
- It does not say the sister is the floor for x > 0. She is the state that *proves* Helike loses; the actual floor
  there is not identified.
- It says nothing yet about SU(3). There Test C showed the lift climbing against her along the wrong circle. Whether
  the cross-reach returns along her own circle is exactly what Test D is measuring.
- The Landau-level and Aharonov–Casher parallels are RESONANCE until I write the map term by term.

### What would test it (an idea, not registered)

- **Prediction:** for x > 0, at large j, the lowest state of every sector is dominated by the counter-rotating type
  (1, −1). Concretely, the block q = l holds the sector floor for all large enough l, with the twist's mirror reading
  for the sister.
- **What would break the click:** a sector at x > 0 where the floor sits in a block with no (1, −1) component. Then
  the reach is not across the body, and the purpose I am reading is wrong.
- This must be pre-registered before it is run, like everything else.
