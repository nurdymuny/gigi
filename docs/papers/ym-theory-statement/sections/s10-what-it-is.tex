\section*{10. What the mass gap is}

\textbf{(a) The theory, and where each of its points stands.} (1)~\emph{The gap has a name}: Helike is proved and cited
(Box~\ref{b:helike}), crossings between the two charges are exact if they occur (Box~\ref{b:cross}), and that the carrier changes only at
the switches is a conjecture (Box~\ref{b:switch}). (2)~\emph{Topology decides who is lightest}: the local harmonic ordering
follows from the reduced Chern--Simons landscape (Box~\ref{b:susy}); its physical interpretation is H8
(Box~\ref{b:h8}, open). (3)~\emph{Tensor and scalar trade
places at a calculable point}: $s_0^*$ is proved at harmonic order (Box~\ref{b:switch0}); the registered $s^*$ are a
conjecture (Box~\ref{b:switch}). (4)~\emph{The fine grain cannot erase the note}: $b_0$ is computed (Box~\ref{b:b0}),
H2 is a conjecture (Box~\ref{b:m3}), and the kinetic contribution is open (Box~\ref{b:kinetic}). (5)~\emph{The infinite world is
the last room}: the scaling reduction and the spectral lemma are proved (Boxes~\ref{b:scaling}, \ref{b:lemma}); H3 and
H4$'$ are conjectures (Boxes~\ref{b:h3}, \ref{b:h4}).

\begin{claim}{CONJECTURE}{The lowest note (the theory)}\label{b:theory}
The mass gap of quantum Yang--Mills theory is the energy of the cheapest topology-leaning excitation, carried by an
identified shape in every room of the sphere family, with exact switches of carrier. The fine grain renormalizes and
dresses the note without erasing it; the large-room limit preserves a uniformly positive $\Delta(R)/\Lambda$.
The $R\to\infty$ end of this family is the $\mathbb R^4$ theory.
\evidence{The geometric and harmonic identities, computed dressing and proposed bridges are separated in Table~1.
The fast logarithm is computed, not the full dressing; H1, H2, H3, H4$'$, H6, H7 and the physical H8 retain their stated standing.}
\lines{11, 12, 17--20, 23, 28}
\end{claim}

\textbf{(b) What is known, and what would be new.} Simulations show a gap with the scalar lightest~\cite{MorningstarPeardon99};
perturbation theory shows asymptotic freedom~\cite{GrossWilczek73,Politzer73}; strong-coupling expansions prove a gap at
the strong-coupling end~\cite{OsterwalderSeiler78}. My theory proposes a geometric connection between them:
leaning toward a change of topology costs energy; the softest local lean is the downhill Chern--Simons direction;
the fine grain dresses a carrier that persists. In this picture they are one mechanism seen from three sides: the
lowest note of the folded space of field shapes, named in the small room and followed to infinite space. The finite-volume road
is L\"uscher's and van Baal's~\cite{Luscher83,KollerVanBaal88,vanBaal00}; the stretched sphere, the identified floor, the
named carrier, the sources, the harmonic switch and the proposed inheritance along scale are the route developed here.

\textbf{(c) Where the description can change.} \emph{$S_C$ fails}: a certified third charge-0 level below $\rho_C$
would remove the ordering argument of Box~\ref{b:order}, not automatically reverse the actual levels.
\emph{Dressing changes the order}: reversing the computed widening would revise \S6; deciding physical H8 requires
the complete splitting of its harmonically tied pair. \emph{No switch where predicted}: a resolved disagreement
would revise the registered finite-coupling prediction, not the exact harmonic formula.
\emph{The carrier loses her identity}: this would change my carrier-inheritance picture even if a positive gap survives.
\emph{$\delta(x)$ grows sublinearly}: every room can be gapped while $\Delta/\Lambda\to0$.
\emph{The $c_0$ tuning fails}: this route would not give the claimed Euclidean last room.
\emph{The short-distance fingerprint changes}: the proposed local-field identification would need revision.
These are ways for the geometry to correct the story, not reasons to stop describing it.

\textbf{(e) What this is.} A complete narrative theory of the Yang--Mills mass gap: complete, because every clause has a
line and every line a place in the story; a theory rather than a proof, with the conjectures visible alongside the
identities and measurements. What I offer here is the shape of the answer: the gap is the lowest note of a
field that cannot change its topology for free. The shape carrying it may change, but the note does not disappear as the room grows.
