## 1. Statement (high fidelity)

**Regulators.** I fix $G=SU(2)$ here; every compact simple $G$ is H7. Let $a_n = 2^{-n}a_0$ (dyadic, so that every reflection hyperplane of $a_n\mathbb Z^4$ is also a lattice hyperplane for all finer $n$; line 22's proof needs this). Let $L\in a_0\mathbb N$ be the physical spatial size. Write $\Lambda_{n,L} = (a_n\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^3\times a_n\mathbb Z$, with periodic space and infinite time. Write $\mu_{n,L}$ for the Wilson measure of line 1 on $\Lambda_{n,L}$ at $\beta_n = 4/g_n^2$. The infinite-time measure exists at fixed $(n,L)$ because the transfer matrix of line 2 is a positive compact operator on $L^2(SU(2)^{\text{spatial links}})$.

**Running of the bare coupling.** I fix the scale nonperturbatively, using a flowed observable (Lüscher 2010). Let $V_s$ be the lattice Wilson flow at flow time $s$, and let $E_s(x)$ be the flowed action density. $g_n$ is defined by
$$
t_0^2\,\langle E_{t_0}(0)\rangle_{\mu_{n,\infty}} = \mathrm{const}
$$
at a fixed physical $t_0$. The perturbative reading of this is the two-loop law
$$
a_n\Lambda_L = \Big(\tfrac{b_0 g_n^2}{16\pi^2}\Big)^{-b_1/(2b_0^2)} e^{-8\pi^2/(b_0 g_n^2)}\,(1+O(g_n^2)),\qquad b_0=\tfrac{22}{3}.
$$
The two-loop factor is not optional. With one-loop running only, a physical length measured in units of $\Lambda^{-1}$ drifts like $(g_n^2)^{-b_1/(2b_0^2)}$, which diverges as $n\to\infty$. Line 20's formula $R\Lambda = e^{-8\pi^2/(b_0g^2(R))}$ is therefore a *definition of $g(R)$ on the sphere*. It is not the bare running for H4.

**Observables.** $\mathcal O$ is the real algebra generated by **flowed gauge-invariant bounded observables**
$$
F = \Phi\big(\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,\mathrm{hol}_{V_t}(\gamma_1),\dots,\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,\mathrm{hol}_{V_t}(\gamma_k)\big),
$$
where $t>0$ is a fixed physical flow time, $\gamma_i$ are piecewise-smooth loops in $\mathbb R^4$ (approximated by lattice loops at each $n$), and $\Phi\in C_b(\mathbb R^k)$. Smeared versions $\int f(x)\,F(\tau_x\cdot)\,dx$ with $f\in C_c^\infty$ are also allowed. I use flowed observables, not bare Wilson loops, because a bare Wilson loop in four dimensions has a perimeter divergence and its expectation tends to $0$ as $a\to0$. Positive flow time removes this, as established to all orders in perturbation theory (Lüscher and Weisz 2011). Every $F\in\mathcal O$ is bounded: $|F|\le\|\Phi\|_\infty$.

**Localisation.** Fix $\delta>0$. Then $\mathcal O_+^{\delta}$ is the class of $F$ whose loops lie in $\{x_0>\delta\}$. $\mathcal O_+ := \bigcup_{\delta>0}\{F\in\mathcal O_+^\delta : t\le t_{\max}(\delta)\}$, where $t_{\max}(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\to 0$ is fixed in Step 4.

**H4 (precise).** Take the order of limits to be time extent $\to\infty$ first (built into $\Lambda_{n,L}$), then $n\to\infty$ at fixed $L$, then $L\to\infty$.

- **(a) Finite-volume UV limit.** For every $L$ and every $F_1,\dots,F_m\in\mathcal O$, $S^{L}(F_1\cdots F_m) := \lim_{n\to\infty}\langle F_1\cdots F_m\rangle_{\mu_{n,L}}$ exists along the full dyadic sequence, not only a subsequence. These limits are the moments of a probability measure $\mu_L$ on a state space $X$ of distributional connections mod gauge, on which the flow is defined (a space of the kind Cao and Chatterjee built in three dimensions, 2021). $\mu_L$ is reflection positive for $\mathcal O_+$ (in the sense of (d)) and invariant under spatial translations and the hypercubic group. Its OS reconstruction gives $(\mathcal H_L, H_L\ge0, \Omega_L)$, with $\Omega_L$ unique.
- **(b) Infinite-volume limit.** $S(F_1\cdots F_m) := \lim_{L\to\infty} S^L(F_1\cdots F_m)$ exists for all $F_i\in\mathcal O$ with compact loop support, and defines a measure $\mu$ on $X$.
- **(c) Euclidean invariance.** $\mu$ is invariant under the full Euclidean group $E(4)=\mathbb R^4\rtimes O(4)$.
- **(d) Reflection positivity.** $S(\Theta(F)F)\ge0$ for all $F\in\mathcal O_+$, where $\Theta$ is time reflection.
- **(e) Clustering.** $S(F\,\tau_{x}G)\to S(F)S(G)$ as $|x|\to\infty$.
- **(f) Non-degeneracy.** $\mu$ is not concentrated on flat connections, and is not Gaussian. This is the minimal content needed for line 26 to be meaningful. The quantitative version is H6.
- **(g) Regularity.** For the renormalised local field $\mathcal E=\mathrm{tr}F^2$ that line 25 (H6) supplies as the limit $t\to0$ of the renormalised $E_t$, the Schwinger distributions satisfy the Osterwalder-Schrader linear growth condition (E0′):
$$
|S_m(f_1,\dots,f_m)| \le \alpha\,(m!)^{\beta}\,\sigma^m\prod_{i=1}^m |f_i|_{s},\qquad f_i\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4),
$$
for some Schwartz norm $|\cdot|_s$ and constants $\alpha,\beta,\sigma$.

**Choice of reading.** Line 23 says "the limit exists, recovers Euclidean symmetry, OS hold, reconstruction gives a relativistic theory." I split it on purpose:

- **(a)–(f)** are measure-level statements about bounded flowed observables. They give a Hilbert space, a Hamiltonian, a vacuum and a unitary Euclidean-to-Poincaré continuation (Fröhlich, Osterwalder and Seiler, Ann. Math. 1983; Klein and Landau 1981). This is everything line 28 uses.
- **(g)** is what makes the reconstruction a *Wightman* theory with local fields. It cannot be stated without the local field of line 25, so H4(g) and H6 have to be proved together.

## 2. Interface

**What other lines take from H4.**

- **Line 28** (gap survives the limit) needs exactly:
  - (i) a limit theory $(\mathcal H,H,\Omega)$ built from RP (this is (a)+(b)+(d));
  - (ii) a set $\mathcal D\subset\mathcal O_+$ with $\{F\Omega\}$ dense in $\mathcal H$. This is automatic, because $\mathcal H$ is *defined* as the completion of $\mathcal O_+$ modulo null vectors;
  - (iii) for each $F\in\mathcal D$ and $\tau>0$, convergence of the truncated two-point function $\langle\Theta F;\tau_{\tau}F\rangle_{L}\to\langle\Theta F;\tau_\tau F\rangle$;
  - (iv) $\sup_{L}\|(F-\langle F\rangle)\Omega_L\|^2<\infty$.

  Item (iv) is **free** for my observable class: $\|(F-\langle F\rangle)\Omega_L\|^2=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_L}(F)\le\|\Phi\|_\infty^2$. So the "constant uniformly bounded" clause of line 28 needs no estimate once the observables are bounded. Line 28's lemma then gives $\int e^{-\tau E}d\nu_F(E)\le\|\Phi\|^2_\infty e^{-c\Lambda\tau}$ for all $\tau$. Hence $\nu_F([0,c\Lambda))=0$, and density gives $\mathrm{spec}\,H\cap(0,c\Lambda)=\emptyset$. **Remark:** line 28 works for *subsequential* limits too. Full uniqueness is needed for line 23's "the limit" and for line 29. It is not needed for the gap bound itself.
- **Line 22** (RP survives limits) is proved for bounded *local* lattice observables with a fixed lattice reflection plane. Flowed observables are not strictly local: the flow spreads support over a radius of order $\sqrt{8t}$. **Line 22 needs something stronger than H4 as worded**: an approximation lemma (SC-loc, Step 4) that makes $\mathcal O_+$ an $L^2$-limit of strictly positive-time lattice observables, uniformly in $n$.
- **Line 27** (unique vacuum) takes clustering (e) and the finite-volume Perron-Frobenius uniqueness.
- **Line 29** (H5) takes uniqueness in (a): the box and the sphere can only "give the same renormalised theory" if each regulator has a unique limit.
- **Lines 25 and 26** take the measure $\mu$ and (f). (g) is shared with H6.

**What H4 takes from other lines.**

- Lines 1–2: the lattice measure and RP [P, PC].
- Line 21: Bałaban's UV stability [PC partial].
- **Line 20 (H3):** the infinite-volume half (b) is not independent of the gap. My route to uniqueness as $L\to\infty$ (Step 5) uses a uniform lower bound $\Delta(L)\ge c\Lambda$ on the torus, which is H3 transported by H5. The order is H3 $\Rightarrow$ H4(b) $\Rightarrow$ line 28. That order is not circular, because line 20 is stated at each finite $R$ and only needs (a).

## 3. What is known

**Proven.**

- Reflection positivity of the Wilson measure, the transfer matrix and $H\ge0$: Osterwalder and Seiler, Ann. Phys. 110 (1978) 440.
- The strong-coupling gap and cluster expansion (line 3) [PC]. This regime is $\beta$ small, which is the opposite of the continuum limit.
- **Bałaban** (Commun. Math. Phys. 1983–89; four-dimensional UV stability in CMP 109 (1987) 249; 116 (1988) 1; 119 (1988) 243; 122 (1989) 175 and 355): a renormalisation-group construction for 4D lattice Yang-Mills in a finite periodic box. It gives upper and lower bounds on the normalised partition function and on effective actions, uniform in $a$. It does **not** give correlation functions, the existence of the limit, the OS axioms, or infinite volume.
- **Magnen, Rivasseau and Sénéor**, Commun. Math. Phys. 155 (1993) 325–383: the Schwinger functions of pure SU(2) YM$_4$ with the UV cutoff removed and a fixed infrared cutoff. They work in the trivial topological sector, with a continuum momentum cutoff, regularised axial gauge and non-perturbative Slavnov identities. The OS axioms are not established, because the IR cutoff stays in place.
- OS reconstruction: Osterwalder and Schrader, CMP 31 (1973) 83 and 42 (1975) 281. The linear growth condition (E0′) is from the 1975 paper.
- Four-dimensional Yang-Mills flow and $\varepsilon$-regularity (small energy in a parabolic ball implies curvature bounds): Struwe, Calc. Var. PDE 1994.
- The O$(a^2)$ structure of lattice pure gauge theory and the classification of dimension-6 operators: Lüscher and Weisz, CMP 97 (1985) 59.
- Gradient flow as a renormalisation-free probe: Lüscher 2010 (JHEP); finiteness to all orders in perturbation theory: Lüscher and Weisz 2011 (JHEP).
- A state space for 3D Yang-Mills built from the flow: Cao and Chatterjee 2021. Stochastic-quantisation constructions of the 2D and 3D Yang-Mills(-Higgs) dynamics: Chandra, Chevyrev, Hairer and Shen, 2022.

**Elementary, and provable now (my claim, not yet written out).** Every $F\in\mathcal O$ is bounded, so for any countable subfamily the joint moments have convergent subsequences (Bolzano-Weierstrass plus a diagonal argument). Hence **subsequential** limits of all $S^L$ and $S$ exist on a countable generating family. They are positive and hypercubic-invariant. All the difficulty sits in four places: uniqueness, RP for the non-local flowed class, O(4), and non-degeneracy.

**Expected but unproven.** Everything in (a)–(g) beyond that.

## 4. Best-guess proof

**Step 1 (fixed $a$, $L$).** The transfer matrix $T_{n,L}=e^{-a_nH_{n,L}}$ is positive and positivity-improving, so the vacuum $\Omega_{n,L}$ is unique (Perron-Frobenius) [PC]. $\mu_{n,L}$ exists, and moments of $\mathcal O$ are defined.

**Step 2 (UV stability, fixed $L$).** Bałaban's block-spin RG at block factor $2$ writes the effective action after $k$ coarsenings as
$$
S_k(U) = \frac{1}{g_k^2}\,S_W(U) + \sum_{X} \mathcal E_k(X;U),\qquad |\mathcal E_k(X)|\le C\, g_k^{\,p}\, e^{-\kappa\, d(X)},
$$
with small-field polymers $\mathcal E_k$ that decay exponentially in the size $d(X)$ of the polymer, and large-field regions suppressed. (I write the polymer bound schematically. The exponent $p>0$ and the precise norms are Bałaban's and are not reproduced here.) The coupling recursion going coarser is
$$
\frac1{g_{k+1}^2} = \frac1{g_k^2} - \frac{b_0}{8\pi^2}\log 2 + O(g_k^2).
$$
This holds uniformly in $n$ at fixed physical $L$ [PC for the partition function and effective actions].

**Step 3 (Cauchy estimate, the key estimate) [guess].** For $F,G\in\mathcal O$ at flow time $t$, and $n'>n$:
$$
\big|\langle FG\rangle_{\mu_{n,L}}-\langle FG\rangle_{\mu_{n',L}}\big| \;\le\; C_{F,G,L}\;\frac{a_n^2}{t}\;|\log a_n|^{p'} .
$$

*Mechanism:*
- Run both lattices down to the common scale $\ell=2^m a_n\sim\sqrt t$.
- The flowed observable depends, up to the locality error of Step 4, only on fields at scales $\ge\sqrt t$.
- The two effective actions at scale $\ell$ have the same marginal term $1/g^2(\ell)$, because the scale is fixed by $t_0$. They differ by irrelevant terms.
- In a hypercubic-invariant pure gauge theory the lowest irrelevant operators have dimension 6 (Lüscher and Weisz 1985). Their effect at scale $\ell$ is $O((a/\ell)^2)$, up to logarithms from their anomalous dimensions.

*Evidence:* Symanzik's effective theory, the all-orders finiteness of flowed observables, and the observed $a^2$ scaling in lattice continuum extrapolations. The estimate is summable over the dyadic sequence ($\sum_n a_n^2|\log a_n|^{p'}<\infty$), so the limit along the full sequence exists. This is (a)'s existence and uniqueness.

**Step 4 (RP for the flowed class; SC-loc) [guess].**

*Sub-conjecture SC-loc:* for $F\in\mathcal O_+^\delta$ at flow time $t$, there are lattice observables $F^{(n)}$ measurable with respect to links in $\{x_0>0\}$ such that
$$
\sup_n\ \|F-F^{(n)}\|_{L^2(\mu_{n,L})}\ \le\ \varepsilon(\delta^2/t),\qquad \varepsilon(u)\to0 \text{ as } u\to\infty .
$$

*Mechanism:*
- The linearised flow is the covariant heat operator plus a curvature term.
- On regions where the flowed field is small at every scale $s\in[a^2,t]$ ($|F(V_s)|\le\varepsilon/s$, which $\varepsilon$-regularity supplies when the local energy at scale $\sqrt s$ is small), the diamagnetic inequality dominates the covariant kernel by the scalar heat kernel, times a factor $e^{O(\varepsilon)\log(t/a^2)}$ that is controlled when $\varepsilon\lesssim g^2(\sqrt s)$.
- This gives dependence on a link at distance $d$ bounded by a Gaussian tail of order $e^{-d^2/(Ct)}$.
- Asymptotic freedom makes the local energy at short scales small *typically*: $s^2\langle E_s\rangle\propto g^2(\sqrt s)\to0$ (Lüscher 2010).
- Large-field regions carry Bałaban's suppression factors.

Given SC-loc, line 22 applies to $F^{(n)}$, and $S(\Theta(F)F)=\lim_{n}\lim \langle\Theta(F^{(n)})F^{(n)}\rangle\ge0$ up to $2\varepsilon\|\Phi\|_\infty$. Letting $\delta^2/t\to\infty$ gives (d). This fixes $t_{\max}(\delta)$.

**Step 5 (infinite volume) [guess, uses H3].**
- The finite-volume gap $\Delta(L)\ge c\Lambda$ (line 20 via line 29) and hypercubic symmetry give exponential clustering in every lattice axis direction, uniformly in $L$: $|\langle\Theta F;\tau_xG\rangle_L|\le\|\Phi_F\|\|\Phi_G\|e^{-c\Lambda|x|_\infty}$ for separations up to $L/2$.
- With a boundary-decoupling estimate of cluster-expansion type, this gives
$$
|S^L(F)-S^{L'}(F)|\le C_F\,e^{-c'\Lambda L},\qquad L'>L,
$$
so the limit exists along the full sequence, and (e) follows. *Evidence:* this is the standard mechanism in every constructed massive theory. The missing piece is volume-uniform large-field control, which Bałaban's bounds do not give.

**Step 6 (O(4) restoration) [guess].** Translation invariance by $a_n\mathbb Z^4$ for every $n$ gives dyadic translations. Continuity of $x\mapsto S(F\,\tau_xG)$ then gives all of $\mathbb R^4$. For rotations, I construct the limit on rotated lattices $R(a_n\mathbb Z^4)$, $R\in SO(4)$, with the same scale-setting at $t_0$. By covariance the limit is $\mu^R=R_*\mu$. **Universality** ($\mu^R=\mu$) is a Step 3-type comparison: after running to scale $\sqrt t$, the two effective theories agree up to dimension-$\ge6$ irrelevant terms, which vanish as $a\to0$. The only gauge-invariant dimension-4 operators are $\mathrm{tr}F^2$ (O(4)-invariant) and $\mathrm{tr}F\tilde F$ (excluded by hypercubic reflections). So nothing marginal can carry anisotropy into the limit. With $\Theta$-symmetry, $\mu$ is then $E(4)$-invariant. This gives (c).

**Step 7 (non-degeneracy).** By construction $t_0^2 S(E_{t_0})=\mathrm{const}>0$, so $\mu$ is not concentrated on flat connections. Non-Gaussianity is H6/line 26: the $1/\log$ short-distance corrections. This gives (f) modulo H6.

**Step 8 (reconstruction).**
- (a)–(e) $\Rightarrow$ $\mathcal H$, $H\ge0$, unique $\Omega$ (line 27), and a unitary Poincaré representation through virtual representations (Fröhlich, Osterwalder and Seiler 1983).
- With (g) for the local field of line 25: OS 1975 $\Rightarrow$ a Wightman field theory.

**Where my programme's pieces enter.** Only lightly, and honestly:
- The heat-trace computation of line 14 (log coefficient $b_0/8\pi^2$ matched to $10^{-4}$, gauge-invariant zeta regularisation) is evidence that the running assigned on the sphere agrees with the lattice running used here. That evidence belongs to H5, not H4.
- The M3 dressing (line 16, H2) is the sphere analogue of Step 3's statement that the fast modes only renormalise the coupling plus irrelevant terms.
- Helike and the competitor play no role in H4.

## 5. Where it can fail

1. **Large fields in growing volume.** Bałaban's large-field suppression is not volume-uniform. If rare large-field regions (for example instanton-like lumps of size $\sim\Lambda^{-1}$) have density that does not cluster, Step 5 fails. *Observed as:* $S^L(F)$ still drifting at $L\gg\Lambda^{-1}$, or several distinct subsequential limits. The latter would contradict line 27.
2. **Non-locality of the flow on rough fields.** If the curvature term in the linearised flow is not controlled on typical configurations, SC-loc fails and RP is not known for $\mathcal O_+$. *Observed as:* flowed correlators $\langle\Theta F\,F\rangle$ that become negative at small $\delta^2/t$, or approach zero only slowly in $\delta^2/t$.
3. **Degenerate limit.** If the scale-setting cannot be held fixed (the $t_0$ condition fails to define $g_n$ monotonically), the limit is free or trivial. The gap of line 28 would then hold vacuously on a one-dimensional $\Omega^\perp$, and line 26 would fail. *Observed as:* $t^2\langle E_t\rangle$ obeying free scaling at all $t$.
4. **Anisotropic universality class.** If an irrelevant operator became marginal non-perturbatively, the limit would remember the hypercubic grain. *Observed as:* flowed Wilson-loop expectations at equal physical size depending on orientation after $a\to0$.
5. **Topology.** On the torus, the topological sectors of the continuum are not defined on rough lattice fields. MRS worked in the trivial sector only. If the $a\to0$ limit at $\theta=0$ did not exist without a sector restriction, (a) would need modification.

## 6. Smallest next step

**Lemma (lattice flow locality, deterministic).** Let $U$ be a lattice configuration on $\Lambda_{n,L}$. Let $V_s(U)$ be its Wilson flow, and suppose the flowed plaquette field satisfies $|F_{\mu\nu}(V_s)(x)|\le \varepsilon/s$ for all $s\in[a_n^2,t]$ and all $x$ in a region $B$. Then there exist $\varepsilon_0, C>0$, independent of $n$, $L$ and $t$, such that for $\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ and lattice points $x\in B$, $y$ (all links involved in $B$):
$$
\big\|\partial_{U_y} V_t(U)_x\big\| \;\le\; C\,\frac{a_n^4}{t^2}\, e^{-|x-y|^2/(Ct)} .
$$

This is the deterministic core of SC-loc. It is the one part of H4 that needs no probability and no RG. Proving it closes RP for the observable class that line 28 uses, conditional only on a probabilistic smallness estimate that Bałaban's small-field bounds already approximate. The larger step, the Cauchy estimate of Step 3, is where most of H4 lives, but it is not the most tractable.
