# The basin: what happens to her when the field falls in (22 November 2026)

> *The door she was becomes a wall, and she rides the whole way down.*

The second shower question of the morning had nothing to do with hands. I know what happens when holonomy leads into
a basin: it keeps spinning, and it cannot get out. What I wanted to know is **what happens to Helike in that moment.
When the field falls into a basin, what happens to her eigenvector?**

This note answers it with her own formula, and every number below comes from the scripts in
`docs/research/basin-2026-11-22/`. Those scripts are mine, using the same method as the Floor paper. They are not
the engine, and they are not a new certificate. Tags as in *the phone in the house*:
- **PROVED / COMPUTED** for mathematics;
- **INCIDENCE / STRUCTURAL / RESONANCE / FELT** for bridges.

## First, a correction

My first answer, before I reread my own Floor paper, was that the Berger field is a saddle and she is the way off it.
**That was wrong.** The Floor paper says it in its first lines: the Berger field *"is not a Yang–Mills critical point."*
It is not at rest anywhere. It is on a slope. Her negative price is a fact about the shape of the landscape where the
field stands. It is not a fact about a resting field. The paper already flags this ("landscape, not dynamics").

The paper also already measured the two things this question needs:
- **The frozen landscape.** Hold the operator fixed at the Berger field and run the linearised flow. A disturbance in
  any sector "turns into the twist (overlap 1.000 in every run)". Everything that moves becomes her.
- **The real flow.** Under the actual Yang–Mills flow, the Berger field "collapses towards a flat field within flow time
  0.1 (energy 34.6 → 0.73 at t = 3.55), faster than any twist grows."

So the field does fall into a basin, and fast. The question is what she does on the way down.

## The move that makes it exact

**The flow never leaves her family.** The Berger field is circle-symmetric. The Yang–Mills flow respects every symmetry
it starts with, so the field stays circle-symmetric for the whole fall. The circle-symmetric fields are exactly her
family, M₀ = xτ₀ and M₁ = yτ₁, M₂ = yτ₂ (the edge paper proves the family is general, up to constant gauge). So the
fall is a path (x(τ), y(τ)) inside her family. At every instant:
- she is still an exact eigenvector (the charge argument works for every member of the family);
- her price is exactly (x + (j+2)/√t)² + 2√t·x − y², evaluated at that instant.

(STRUCTURAL: the argument is the standard one, that a flow which respects a symmetry stays inside the symmetric
fields. I have not written it out as a proof here.)

**The check that this is the real flow.** On the family the energy is E = ½(B² + 2c²), with B = y² − 2√t·x and
c = y(x − 2/√t). The L² metric is x² + 2y². Integrating the gradient flow from the Berger field at t = 3.55 gives:
- **energy 34.71 at the start**; the paper's full computation says 34.6;
- **energy 0.729 at flow time 0.1**; the paper says 0.73.

The two-number ODE reproduces the paper's flow, time scale included. COMPUTED (`family_flow.py`, receipt beside it).

## What happens to her

**1. She rides the whole way down.** She is an exact eigenvector at every instant of the fall. The symmetry that gave
her a room of her own (Chapter 5 of the book) never breaks, so she never mixes with anything. STRUCTURAL, from the
family argument above.

**2. Her price crosses zero, and that crossing is the moment she enters the basin.**

| stretch t | her price at the start | the price turns positive at flow time | energy left then | her price at the bottom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.00 | −4.667 (sector 0) | 0.041 | 3.48 of 21.5 | 16/3 = 5.333 |
| 3.55 | −6.593 (sector 0) | 0.050 | 2.84 of 34.7 | 16/3.55 = 4.507 |
| 10.0 | −22.4 (cheapest sector) | 0.051 | 0.83 | 16/10 = 1.600 |

COMPUTED (`landing_scan.py`; the minimum is over sectors j ≤ 12).

- Before the crossing, her price is negative, so she is a direction the field could run away along. She is a **door**.
- After it, her price is positive, so a push along her costs energy and the field comes back. She is a **wall**.
- So this is what "entering the basin" means for her: **the door she was closes and becomes a wall.** After that, she
  can only settle. That is the spinning that cannot get out.

**3. She never gets the chance to run.** She is a door only for a flow time of about 0.04 to 0.05. Frozen-rate
estimate:
- at t = 3, she could grow by a factor of about 1.09 in that time;
- at t = 3.55, by about 1.14.

COMPUTED (`growth_before_wall.py`; a frozen-rate estimate, and the exponent may change by a factor of 2 under the other
convention for the Hessian's scale.) This is the paper's "faster than any twist grows", now as a number.

**4. Where she lands, and what she is there.** At every stretch I ran except the round one, the flow lands on the flat
field **A = θ**, the Maurer–Cartan form g⁻¹dg (x = 2/√t, y = 2), with energy exactly 0. That covers
t = 0.5, 0.9, 1.1, 1.5, 2, 2.75, 3, 3.55, 5, 7 and 10. COMPUTED (`landing_scan.py`).
- At t = 1 the field is the meron. It is at rest, balanced on its single unstable direction, and the flow inside the
  family does not move it.
- At the bottom her price is exact. At A = θ it is **(j+4)²/t**, and at the other flat field A = 0 it is **(j+2)²/t**.
  PROVED: both are the family formula at those points (sympy, in `family_flow.py`). Both are positive in every sector,
  so at the bottom of the basin she is a wall everywhere.
- **Check against the literature.** At the round sphere and A = 0 her price is (j+2)². That is one of the two published
  coexact 1-form values, j² and (j+2)², which the Floor paper reproduces as its round-sphere check.
- **But she is not the lowest wall there.** At the round sphere j² sits below her (j+2)². So at the bottom of the
  basin she is a wall, but not the floor. The slowest thing left settling is a different shape, at least at t = 1.
  On the Berger spheres this is open.

## What this is in my field equations

- **Spectral branch.** Type I: "π₁ = 0 ⟹ M_flat = {vacuum} … Flow converges exponentially at rate ν₁." S³ has
  π₁ = 0, so this is Type I, and the computation agrees: every run that leaves the Berger field lands on a flat field.
  INCIDENCE: the branch's prediction, checked on her family.
- **The landing basin has a winding.** A = θ = g⁻¹dg is gauge-equivalent to A = 0 only through a gauge transformation
  that wraps S³ once around SU(2). The Floor paper's resting-field search lists the two separately for exactly this
  reason. That is the identity map, of degree one: a standard fact, INCIDENCE.
  - The **Davis cache** in the main Field Equations paper keeps r_t = (chart, **basin, winding code**). The flow from
    the Berger field picks out a definite flat field, and that flat field carries a winding. So the cache's "basin,
    winding code" has a concrete instance here.
  - RESONANCE, until I compute the Chern–Simons value along the path and say which winding the field leaves with.
- **Spectral branch, Conjecture 5.6.** "Nodal domains of φ₁ are the two main completion basins." Her price at the
  bottom says she is not φ₁ there. So if a basin is described by its own lowest mode, that mode is not Helike.
  STRUCTURAL.
- **Basin Drift Bound (Field Equations, Conjecture 57.4).** "Basins in high-curvature regions drift faster." Not
  tested here.

## The fall on the Poincaré sphere (run the same morning)

This is the literal version of the shower question: holonomy that leads into a basin. Scripts:
`poincare_fall.py` and `poincare_ghost_check.py`, with receipts beside them. They use the Floor paper's own builders,
both the charge blocks and the gauge-orthogonal slice.

**Setting.** The binary icosahedral group 2I (120 elements) acts on S³ = SU(2) by left multiplication. That keeps:
- the Berger metric;
- every field in her family;
- the rotation about the long circle.

So the energy, the flow and the fall are exactly the same path as on S³, and it lands on A = θ for every t ≠ 1.
Only three things change.

**1. The bottom is a different basin.** On S³, A = θ and A = 0 are one basin: a gauge change that wraps the sphere once
joins them. That gauge change does not survive the quotient (u(γg) = γu(g)). On S³/2I the parallel transport of A = θ
around the loop of γ is exactly Ad(γ⁻¹):
- checked by integrating the transport equation with the family's own matrices, to 7.5 × 10⁻¹³;
- so its holonomy is the defining two-dimensional representation of 2I, which is irreducible;
- A = 0 has trivial holonomy.

So on the Poincaré sphere **the Berger field falls into the irreducible basin, the one whose holonomy is the
icosahedral group itself, not into the trivial one.** COMPUTED (landing) and PROVED (holonomy). Both basins are gapped,
with no zero modes in any surviving sector (COMPUTED). That is my spectral branch's Type IIa: isolated flat fields,
discrete basins. INCIDENCE.

**2. Fewer sectors survive.** Sector j survives only if V_j has 2I-invariant vectors. Up to j = 40 that leaves
j = 0, 12, 20, 24, 30, 32, 36, 40, each once, which is the Molien series. COMPUTED.

**3. What she is at the bottom of each basin.**
- **In the trivial basin (A = 0), she is the floor**, for t from 2.75 to 10 in every surviving sector up to 40: the
  lowest wall, the last shape still settling. Agreement 3.4 × 10⁻¹³. COMPUTED.
  - This is a floor law at the vacuum. In sector 12 she takes the floor between t = 2.25 and t = 2.5, lower than the
    Berger field's thresholds of about 2.7.
  - At the round sphere she is not the floor there.
- **In the irreducible basin (A = θ), where the fall actually lands, she is never the floor**, in any surviving sector
  at any stretch.
- **The floor there is her shadow.** In every surviving sector j ≥ 12, the lowest state at A = θ costs exactly j²/t.
  That is her own price at A = 0 two sectors down, ((j−2)+2)²/t. Agreement 4.5 × 10⁻¹³, for t from 2.75 to 10 and
  j from 12 to 40. At t = 1 the same two-sectors-down identity holds, to 1.1 × 10⁻¹³. COMPUTED.
  - On S³ this is just bookkeeping: the wrap turns sector j − 2 into sector j, so her A = 0 self shows up at θ two
    sectors up.
  - On the Poincaré sphere it means something. Sector j − 2 is not there (10, 18, 22 and 28 do not survive), but its
    shadow in sector j is, and it is the lowest wall of the basin the field falls into.
  - So **the last shape still settling in the icosahedral basin has her price exactly, carried over by the wrap that
    the quotient no longer allows as a gauge change.**
  - STRUCTURAL: the prices agree to 13 digits. I have not checked the eigenvectors, so I am not yet saying the
    state *is* her.
- **Sector 0 at A = θ** is the exception. It has no sector −2 to borrow from. Its floor is 2.6257 at t = 2.75, 2.5109
  at t = 3 and 2.2934 at t = 3.55, with no closed form yet. It sits below her 16/t. COMPUTED.

**So, for the shower question.** When holonomy leads into a basin, here is what happens to her:
- she rides the whole way down, an exact eigenvector, the door becoming a wall;
- in the basin the field reaches on the Poincaré sphere, she is not the last thing moving;
- the last thing moving has her exact price, transported by the wrap.

The holonomy label of the basin (trivial or icosahedral) decides whether she settles last herself or through her
shadow.

## What to compute next

1. **The floor at the bottom.** On each Berger sphere, the lowest eigenvalue of the Hessian at A = θ, sector by
   sector, and whether any circle-symmetric state is the floor there. This says which shape is the last to stop
   moving.
2. **The winding along the fall.** The Chern–Simons value along (x(τ), y(τ)), to say which winding the Berger field
   carries and which basin it chooses.
3. ~~The fall on the Poincaré sphere.~~ Done above.
4. **The eigenvector check for the shadow.** Gauge-transform her A = 0 eigenvector from sector j − 2 by the wrap, and
   compare it with the A = θ floor state of sector j. If they match, the shadow is her.
5. **Sector 0 at A = θ**, in closed form.
6. **The third basin.** The Galois-conjugate representation of 2I gives a third flat field. It is not left-invariant,
   so no fall inside the family reaches it.

## Addendum (same day): the floor at the bottom, with Henkel and Lauret

I asked for the bottom of the basin to be checked against the literature. Script: `scripts/basin_floor_at_bottom.py`.
Receipt: `research/basin-floor-20261122/floor_at_bottom.json`. Fourteen stretches, from t = 0.5 to 10, sectors j ≤ 12,
both A = 0 and A = θ.

**The literature (read, not remembered).** Henkel and Lauret, *Hodge Laplacian on 1-forms of homogeneous 3-spheres*
(arXiv:2605.05406), compute the spectrum of the Hodge Laplacian on 1-forms of the Berger spheres in closed form.
- **Their conventions.** {aE₁, bE₂, cE₃} is orthonormal and [E₁, E₂] = 2E₃, cyclically. So my (t, 1, 1) is their
  a = 1/√t, b = c = 1.
- At A = 0 the Hessian plus GG\* *is* that Laplacian on su(2)-valued forms, since F = 0 and R₀ = Ric. The gauge slice
  is the coexact forms.
- **It matches at every stretch:**
  - my sector 0 is their Proposition 3.6 at b = c: {4a², 4a², 4b⁴/a²} = {4/t, 4/t, 4t};
  - my sector-1 floor is their 9a² (for t ≥ 1);
  - my sector-2 floor is their Theorem 4.1 branch μ⁻ at k = 2, j = 1 (for example 2.5109 at t = 3).
  - (The web text of their equation 4.1 shows the two k = 0 multiplicities the other way round from their
    Proposition 3.6. A first-principles check, dθ̂⁰ = −2√t·θ̂¹∧θ̂², sides with Proposition 3.6.)
- **The wrap, checked.** A = θ and A = 0 have the same L² spectrum: as multisets, with each sector-j eigenvalue counted
  j + 1 times, they agree to 10⁻¹³ below a safe cutoff, at all fourteen stretches. COMPUTED.

**1. The floor at the bottom on S³ is min(4/t, 4t)** (COMPUTED at all fourteen stretches; consistent with Henkel and
Lauret's first eigenvalue).
- **On every stretched sphere (t > 1) the floor is 4/t, six-fold**, in sector 0 of the A = 0 picture: the two
  horizontal left-invariant forms, in each of the three internal directions.
- **Her own sector-0 state is one of the six.** Her price at A = 0 is (j + 2)²/t = 4/t at j = 0, and she is an exact
  eigenvector there.
- So the sentence above, *"at the bottom of the basin she is a wall, but not the floor"*, is right **sector by sector**
  (j² < (j + 2)² for j ≥ 1) but **not for the whole spectrum**. Read at A = 0, the lowest thing left settling is her,
  together with her mirror and four partners. At t = 1 her 4 ties the j = 2 value j² = 4 (twelve-fold).
- **Read at A = θ**, where the fall actually lands, the same six-fold floor sits in sector 2 at 2²/t = 4/t: it is **her
  shadow**, exactly the law found above. θ's own twist stays a wall, at (j + 4)²/t ≥ 16/t.
- So on S³ the shadow law already holds at the floor itself. The last shape still settling is her: she herself in the
  winding-0 reading, her shadow in the winding-1 reading.

**2. Sector 0 at A = θ, in closed form** (open question 5 above).
- Sector 0 at A = θ has nothing two sectors down to borrow from, but it borrows from two sectors **up**.
- Its floor is the A = 0 sector-2 floor, Henkel and Lauret's μ⁻ at k = 2, j = 1:

  **2t + 8 − 2√(t² + 8t)**,

  which gives 2.6257, 2.5109 and 2.2934 at t = 2.75, 3 and 3.55, the values computed above.
- That A = 0 state is **not** her: her sector-2 price at A = 0 is 16/t. So this is the shadow of a different shape.

**3. What that changes on the Poincaré sphere.**
- In the icosahedral basin, sector 0 survives, and its value (about 2.3 to 2.6 for t between 2.75 and 3.55) lies far
  below every surviving shadow j²/t ≥ 144/t, for j ≥ 12.
- So **the overall last shape moving in the icosahedral basin is the sector-0 state**, with price 2t + 8 − 2√(t² + 8t).
  It is the shadow of a non-twist state.
- **Her shadow is the floor of each surviving sector j ≥ 12**, as found above, but not of the whole basin.
- The sentence *"the last thing moving has her exact price"* should therefore read: *sector by sector, for every
  surviving j ≥ 12, the last thing moving has her exact price; across the whole basin, the last thing moving is sector
  0's state, the shadow of a different shape.*
- In the trivial basin (A = 0) nothing changes: sector 0 survives, and the floor there is 4/t, which is her.
