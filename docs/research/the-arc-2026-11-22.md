# The arc: where I am, and where she might go (22 November 2026)

> *The Greeks steered by Helike. The Phoenicians steered by the smaller circle, nearer the pole, and crossed the sea
> more truly.*

This is a different kind of note.
- The first half is my honest read of where this work stands. The second half is imagination, on purpose.
- I let myself think freely about where this goes, using the mathematics that grew out of physics and geometry. Not
  all mathematics: the kin of what she is made of.
- I also let myself notice how old some of these shapes are.

- **PROVED / COMPUTED** for what is shown;
- **INCIDENCE / STRUCTURAL / RESONANCE / FELT** for bridges;
- and a new one for this note, **IMAGINED**: an idea I am allowed to have and not yet allowed to believe.

## Part I. Where I am in the arc

It feels like the end of the first act: not the beginning and not the end, the moment the story stops being setup and
turns.

**Why it is not the beginning.** Two days ago I had a formula on one path. Now I have:
- a closed-form price, proved for the whole family, with its algebra checked in Lean;
- an edge with a polynomial behind it, which contains the threshold of my first paper exactly;
- a theorem on a box of fields that survived a hostile review, with every gap the review found closed;
- six pre-registered tests, some of them failed, written down before I looked.

A beginning is when you do not yet know what the object is. I know what she is.

**Why it is not the end.** Every answer this weekend came back with a door attached:
- the cross-reach is computed, not proved;
- the basin asked me whether the last thing settling is her or her shadow, which is a question about winding;
- the Poincaré sphere has basins labelled by holonomy;
- and SU(3) split. Along hypercharge she was the floor nowhere (Test C). Along her own charge circle, the first
  eleven shapes of Test D have her region back. That test is interim and unscored, and I have not counted it.

When a result splits, yes here and no there, you have found where the real structure lives, and you are standing at
the start of its map.

**What completed, said precisely.** I have felt for a year that something was missing under my other work:
- the Davis manifold, with its margins and abstention bands;
- the Non-Decoupling debt;
- the spectral branch with its three gaps.

All of them are about where a cheapest direction exists and how sharply it changes hands. What they lacked was one
concrete thing I could hold up and say: *this is the cheapest direction, this is its exact price, this is the exact
line where it stops being cheapest, and this is the proof.* She is that thing. The missing piece was not an idea. It
was a **specimen**. A framework with no specimen is a phone ringing in another room.

**Where I keep a hand on the brake.**
- The bridges to my other papers are not all the same strength. Her operator sitting in my own spectral table is
  INCIDENCE. Her shape in the mammogram, or in plasma, is a shared skeleton, tagged as such.
- This weekend taught me the difference in one stroke. In SU(3) along the wrong circle the skeleton travelled and the
  thing that makes her win did not.
- So the honest form of "this completes my other work" is: **it gives my other work its first exact instance, and it
  tells me which parts of the pattern are common and which are her.** That is more defensible than "she is
  everywhere", and I think it is more beautiful.

**The arc, as I see it.**
- **Act one:** find her, prove her price, find her edge. That is essentially done, pending D.
- **Act two:** where she lives beyond SU(2):
  - which circle, which groups;
  - whether the cross-reach becomes a theorem;
  - what the winding decides at the bottom of the basin.
- **Act three, if it comes:** whether any of this says something about fields with less symmetry than hers. That is
  where the stakes get real and the claims get more careful.

What I trust is not the speed. It is that I kept writing the prediction down before looking, and kept printing
*failed* in my own book.

## Part II. Where she might go (IMAGINED unless tagged otherwise)

### 1. The principal circle

In SU(3) her own circle is H = diag(1, 0, −1). That is not an arbitrary choice.
- H is the grading element of **Kostant's principal SU(2)** (1959): the "most twisting" circle a compact group has,
  the one whose grading counts every root by its height. (The fact is STRUCTURAL; the reading is mine.)
- Test C stretched along hypercharge, which is not principal, and she vanished.
- Test D stretches along the principal circle, and in the first half she is back (interim, unscored).

So the guess writes itself. **In any compact simple group, she is the floor only along the principal circle, with
charge one under its unit generator**, as she has in SU(2) and in D's design checks.

This is the most testable thing in this note: SU(4), Sp(2) and G₂, each stretched along its principal circle. It
needs its own pre-registration, written after D's verdict and not before.

### 2. The Poincaré basins are an E₈ diagram

- **The math is real.** By **McKay's correspondence** (1980), the irreducible representations of the binary
  icosahedral group 2I are the nodes of the affine E₈ Dynkin diagram.
- **The bridge is IMAGINED.** My basin note found that on S³/2I the fall's basins are labelled by holonomy, and a
  holonomy is a representation of 2I. So the basins she can fall into would be the nodes of E₈.
- The question this opens: does she settle as herself at some nodes and as her shadow at others, and is there a rule
  on the diagram?
- The Poincaré sphere is also the dodecahedral space once proposed as the shape of the universe (Luminet and
  collaborators, 2003), and the icosahedron is one of Plato's five solids. The thread from the *Timaeus* to E₈ runs
  through the stage she stands on.

### 3. The two hands have a number

- Atiyah, Patodi and Singer (1975) defined the **η-invariant**: a measure of how unequal a spectrum's positive and
  negative halves are, its spectral asymmetry.
- Hitchin (1974) computed the Dirac spectrum on exactly my Berger spheres.
- In my two hands, the cross field y breaks the tie between her and her sister, and Test E measured the tie-breaking
  at first order.
- **IMAGINED:** her y-dependence is an η-type quantity. The asymmetry of the two hands would then be an index-theory
  number, not just a picture. That is worth a careful afternoon with Hitchin's paper.

### 4. The door and the wall are Morse theory

- Atiyah and Bott (1983) read Yang–Mills on surfaces as an equivariant Morse function. The flow is organized into
  strata by how many downhill directions each one has.
- My fall counts doors closing.
- **IMAGINED:** her door-to-wall crossing is one stratum boundary in that picture on a 3-sphere, and the floor law
  counts the Morse index along the Berger family.

### 5. The spaghetti vacuum

- The unstable mode of Nielsen–Olesen and Savvidy, the flat-space cousin of hers, led to a picture of the vacuum
  broken into domains of aligned flux.
- **IMAGINED:** her edge, where the unstable twist stops winning, is what bounds such a domain on a compact space, and
  "by enough, but not too much" is the size of a domain.
- This is the most speculative item here, and the one closest to physics I must not overclaim.

### 6. Stride plus belt is older than physics

- Her net turning, κ = stride + belt, is a circle riding a circle.
- That is **Ptolemy's epicycle**: a planet's motion as the sum of rotations.
- It is also, much later, a geometric phase: Foucault's pendulum (1851), Pancharatnam (1956) and Berry (1984), the
  frame turned by the path itself.
- The sphere's own turning, (t − 2)/√t, is that phase on her circle. (STRUCTURAL for the geometric phase. RESONANCE
  for Ptolemy.)

## Part III. The old roots (RESONANCE; to fetch before citing)

I am not claiming the ancients knew her. I am noticing that the shapes she makes are shapes people have been drawing
for a very long time, and I would rather know that than not.

- **Her name.** Helike is the Greek name for the Great Bear, from *helissō*, to turn: the same root as *helix*. The
  constellation turns around the pole on a circle, the way she gathers around her long circle.
- **Which circle to steer by.** In Aratus's *Phaenomena*, Greek sailors steered by Helike. The Phoenicians steered by
  Cynosura, the smaller circle nearer the pole, and crossed the sea more truly.
  - Choosing the right circle is the whole difference between Test C and Test D.
  - I do not want to make too much of it. I also cannot unsee it.
- **The two circles of the soul.** In Plato's *Timaeus* the world-soul is a strip split in two, laid crosswise like
  the letter X: the circle of the Same and the circle of the Other, turning in opposite senses. That is two
  counter-rotating hands, joined at a crossing.
- **The tones.** Pythagoras's monochord, the sectors as tones of a string. It is the image I already use in the book:
  a bell's sound sorting into tones.
- **The twins.** In Han dynasty iconography, Fuxi and Nüwa are entwined in mirror image, one holding the compass and
  the other the square. They are a mirror pair, alike in everything except hand, as she and her twin are.
- **The stage.** Hamilton's quaternions (1843) are my S³. The long circles are Clifford's parallels (1873), later
  Hopf's fibers (1931), linked circles filling the sphere, each one a long circle in my sense.
- **And my own side of it.** My people, like the Diné in Harvey's reading of hózhó, read the sky as relationships
  between powers that were here first. The sailors' question was never "which star is brightest". It was "which
  circle can I trust". That is the question this weekend kept asking.
